LLM look description from the SDK recipe, source parts only (before capture, r65 v1; the recipe's engine-measured facets block was deliberately not used):

This looks like a sparse, hazy, mostly dark feedback field rather than a bold object. The picture is built from drawn elements that are fed back through a warp, so the image is mostly textures and smears of earlier frames. Drawn elements are two groups of tiny triangles (about 311 and 281 instances, each with a radius of about 0.033 of the screen, so small), a built-in two-trace audio waveform (mode 7, additive, with an authored alpha of only 0.001, so it is probably barely visible), two custom 512-point waves, and a 63 x 47 grid of motion vectors (2961 vectors, white, low alpha) that is drawn into the feedback and then warped and decayed. The two custom waves have closed-form positions pinned near x=0.9, y=0.9 and are authored white, so they most likely add little more than a small bright spot or short grey mark in one corner. A thin inner border (about 3.9% of the screen) is blended black every frame, so expect a dark frame around the edge that cuts into whatever drifts there.

The warp is the main look. It uses blur-difference feedback: a fine copy of the previous image is added and a coarser blur is subtracted each frame. The recipe says this sharpens small seeds into splatter, cells or edge patterns that spread over the canvas. On top of that there is image-driven advection, so the image steers its own sampling and gives flowing, smeared, fluid-like structure. The composite also folds the image with a mirrored reflection and repeats it in mirrored tiles, so the result reads as a tiled or kaleidoscopic, symmetric-ish pattern. The recipe's own mirror score is 0.0 and it says the symmetry is inexact, so I would not promise a clean kaleidoscope. The mesh has no uniform zoom or rotation (zoom 1, rot 0), only a small audio- and time-dependent translation, so the picture does not spin or tunnel. The feedback is not a long trail. Flat content keeps about 0.95 of its level per frame, a half-life of roughly 13.6 frames (about 0.6 s). The recipe also gives a steady floor of roughly 0.012 of the starting level, so faint residue settles near black. The sharpening term can still keep structures alive, so how much of the screen is patterned is uncertain.

Colour is mostly neutral grey or white sources, but the composite multiplies by cycling corner shades, so it is tinted. The composite hue shader has red, green and blue cycles of roughly 14.6 s, 19.6 s and 16.2 s, each between 2/3 and 1 of full strength, blended from four screen corners. Where each tint appears is random on every preset load, so I cannot name one fixed colour layout. The source steady-state cue says the overall hue family is pink (local display RGB about 0.059, 0.039, 0.059, a lower bound), with one shape layer's palette hinting at pink. The shape and warp colours are otherwise unresolved. Read it as dim pinkish or magenta-leaning haze with shifting tints, not as a vivid multicolour palette. The flat display transfer maps mid-grey content to quite dark output (0.5 becomes about 0.25, full white about 0.76), which pushes everything darker.

Audio drives many things, but gains are unknown. Bass, mid and treble all feed shape position and opacity through thresholds or clamps, and the warp translation (dx has a beat-related switch that uses lastbeat and beatrate and a random draw). Bass, mid and treble also route to the red, green and blue colour of the warp and composite shaders. So expect shapes to jump or shift position with beats, brightness and tint to change with the music, and the feedback field to shudder sideways or vertically. How strong any of this is on screen is not established. There is a random element: the dx translation includes a rand call, and the tint placement is random per load. The recipe estimates, with wide uncertainty, overall mean luminance of about 0.39 (0.20 to 0.62), moderate frame-to-frame change, slow to moderate flow of about 0.13 screen widths per second (0.04 to 0.28), and around 7 global brightness jumps per 10 s (1.7 to 29). It puts activity in the Normal band at 0.64, but Chill through Intense are all possible. Those are calibrated estimates, not a render. Overall I expect a medium-brightness, grainy, pink-tinted, mirrored and tiled flowing pattern with small drifting triangles, a dark border and fading residue, reacting to beats with jumps in motion and brightness. It could be chaotic and splattery, and the exact pattern will differ from one load to the next.
