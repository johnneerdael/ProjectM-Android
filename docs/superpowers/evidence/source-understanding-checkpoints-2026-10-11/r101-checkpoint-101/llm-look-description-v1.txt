LLM look description from the SDK recipe, source parts only (before capture, r101 v1; the recipe's engine-measured facets block was deliberately not used):

Core picture. "Various - Fire and Brimstone" is a feedback-driven preset: most of what is on screen is a self-evolving, smeared, organic texture rather than a few drawn objects. The warp shader combines several mechanisms (render.mechanisms): a finer-minus-coarser blur difference (pattern_growth, lane 1, fine level 0 and coarse level 3, flat gain 1.0), gradient-driven advection (fluid or reaction-diffusion-like flow), a two-direction relief stage (embossed edges, flat areas read as one level) and two image-driven self-advection stages with sample offsets up to about 0.06-0.08 UV. Seeds grow into blobs, cells and splatter that spread over the canvas, so by 5 seconds I expect the whole screen to be patterned, flowing and liquid-looking, like churning embers or lava. The drawn layers add very little. The three quad shapes (4 sides, radius 0.1, one instance each) are centred at source y = 1.8, which with y=0 at the top lies well outside the visible frame, so they probably contribute nothing. The built-in wave (mode 7, two parallel traces from the left and right waveform) sits at y = 0.96, close to the bottom edge, with an authored alpha of only 0.001, so at most a very faint thin line along the bottom. The recipe does not resolve whether either layer is visible. The composite is an identity (flat grey ramp, output equals input), so the look comes entirely from the feedback canvas.

Colour. The recipe gives one real colour cue: render.feedback.steady_state has hue_family "yellow", with a settled canvas/display colour of about RGB (0.96, 0.96, 0.08), a lower bound for brightness. The warp transfer explains this. Red keeps nearly all its level (gain 0.994, steady level 1.0), green is mostly kept (gain 0.97, nonlinear, steady 1.0), and blue is cut hard (gain 0.85, halving in about 4.3 frames, steady level only 0.053). So the canvas drifts towards red plus green, which is yellow, orange and amber, with blue suppressed. That fits a fire-and-brimstone look: yellow-orange-amber flame tones, possibly with reddish areas where green is lower. The calibrated saturation is high, about 0.72 (interval 0.44-0.99), so the colours should be vivid rather than washed out. Warm versus cool is only weakly estimated (0.39, interval 0.06-0.75, not decisive), so I rely on the yellow steady-state cue and the blue suppression instead. The source colours of the shapes and warp are listed as unresolved and the wave is plain grey, so the recipe does not know where reds or darker shades would appear. The wave's base grey is 0.5 and, because of max-normalisation above 0.01, would be drawn as white, if it were visible at all.

Motion. Every feedback step contracts the image toward the screen centre by about 0.145-0.149% (zoom 0.99853, zoomexp 1.01) and rotates it 0.002 rad (about 0.11 degrees). The centre of that zoom and rotation wanders: cx ranges 0.27-0.73 and cy 0.17-0.83 on slow sinusoids of time. On top there is a small, finely structured translation field (dx up to about 0.002, dy about -0.006 to +0.003) built from high-frequency sines of x and y (the 88 and 888 factors) that shimmer over time. The shader advection adds fluid-like smearing. Overall I expect a slow inward-drifting, gently swirling flow with wandering pivot, with fluid or boiling texture on top, not fast jumps. The calibrated estimate puts mean flow at about 0.09 screen widths per second (interval 0.014-0.22) and mean frame-to-frame difference at 0.035 (interval 0.005-0.079). I read that as a low-to-moderate pace. Contraction toward the centre means new detail should appear to flow inward. The recipe does not give the trail persistence in time (half-life unknown), only the per-channel gains above.

Brightness and contrast. The display estimate gives mean luminance about 0.43 (interval 0.24-0.67), so a mid-bright picture that could plausibly be fairly dark or fairly bright. Red and green persist at near full level while blue fades, so lit areas should be bright yellow, and the steady-state lower bound of about 0.96 for red and green hints that large areas could saturate toward bright yellow. The relief and blur-difference stages add edge contrast and texture, so the picture should not be flat. Brightness jumps are estimated at 0 per 10 s (interval up to about 2.5). The recipe finds no flash mechanism, so I expect no strobing and a steady brightness. The calibrated activity is low (0.086, band Chill, but the interval 0.002-0.73 allows Normal or Intense). The source-only mood layer says intensity about 38 (interval 38-100), smoothness about 68, tag "Organic / Liquid", with low confidence.

Audio response. The only audio route in the recipe is the built-in waveform (left and right channels) shaping the two parallel traces, which is nearly invisible at alpha 0.001. There are no bass, mid or treble bindings (audio_bindings is empty) and no audio brightness route. So I do not expect beat-synchronised pulses, zooms or colour changes. The look should be mostly the same whatever the music, and any music link would be indirect, through what the waveform line deposits into the feedback. The preset also redraws q2 to q7 at random (random_redraws), with unknown timing (event times and period are null). Whatever those values drive, such as the shader warp or colour terms, will change in an unpredictable way over time without being tied to the music.

Uncertainty. The recipe is unsure about: which layers are visible (shapes off-frame, wave nearly transparent); the actual palette beyond the yellow lean (sources and feedback colours unresolved; border colours are None, though borders cover 2.0% outer and 3.9% inner of the screen and blend every frame, so there may be a thin frame of some colour at the edges); how dense or bright the grown patterns get; the real trail persistence and half-life; the q2 to q7 random effects, which could change the look from load to load or over time; and the Chill versus Normal activity band. Mean luminance has a wide interval (0.24-0.67), so overall brightness could be off by a lot. Whether the near-saturated yellow floor fills the whole screen, or only the brightest regions, is not established. All motion rates are per-step source facts, not measured speed. The calibrated statistics are estimates from static facts, not a measurement of this preset.
