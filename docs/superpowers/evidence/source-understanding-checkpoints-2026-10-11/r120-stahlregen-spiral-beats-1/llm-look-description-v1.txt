LLM look description from the SDK recipe, source parts only (before capture, r120 v1, core recipe view; the recipe's engine-measured facets block was deliberately not used):

Approximate look after about 5 seconds: a dense, tunnel-like feedback picture. A single audio-driven waveform line is drawn over a persistent canvas, and the whole canvas is continuously pulled toward the screen centre, so the line leaves behind receding, repeating copies that stream inward. Because the composite adds a left-right and a top-bottom mirrored copy at 50% opacity, the result should look four-fold symmetric, kaleidoscope-like, about the centre, with the symmetry upper estimate at 1.0 (inexact in practice because the echo zoom is 1.007, the warp centre is offset at cx 0.65, cy 0.35, and later drawing breaks it).

Core picture. Three drawn ingredients feed the feedback. (1) A built-in waveform of mode 6 (angled trace, non-dotted, thin, non-additive), position 1.0/1.0, following the left-channel audio waveform. It is the main visible stroke, and its alpha is about 1.006, so it is fully opaque. (2) One four-sided polygon (a square or diamond) with a radius of 1.265 in NDC units, centred at 0.5/0.5. It is larger than the screen, so it is clipped and may act as a large near-white wash or panel (fill r/g/b 1.0, alpha 0.9, border alpha 0, texture zoom 0.65). Whether it shows as a broad bright field is unresolved. Its rotation is audio-dependent and otherwise unknown. (3) The feedback warp. Every frame the previous image is sampled so that content contracts toward the centre by about 3.4% to 4.65% per step (zoom 0.961, zoomexp 1.149, so the rate varies with radius). Rotation, dx and dy are all 0, so there is no sideways drift and no spin from the warp itself. A tiny deformation term (warp 0.01) adds very slight wobble. The effect is a smooth zoom-in tunnel, not a swirl. No random textures are bound.

Colour. The wave colour shifts over time: each of r, g and b ranges roughly 0.1 to 0.9, and it is then max-normalised so the brightest channel is always full. The line is therefore a saturated, hue-shifting colour, and bass modulates its r, g and b. Which hues it actually reaches is unverified. The recipe's steady-state estimate for the overall picture is grayscale (about 0.81 display level, canvas about 0.45), which suggests that bright, near-white or grey content, likely the large white polygon, sits behind or between the coloured wave trails. The calibrated estimate gives mean saturation about 0.64 (interval 0.36 to 0.91) and a neutral warm/cool balance (about -0.04, interval -0.38 to +0.31). My reading is a mix of pale or white backing with coloured, hue-shifting wave streaks. The palette is uncertain, and I would not call it strongly warm or cool.

Motion. The warp contracts the image continuously, and flow is estimated at about 0.15 screen widths per second (interval 0.05 to 0.32), so it is moderate. The estimated mean frame-to-frame change is about 0.025 (0 to 0.066), which is low to modest. The source puts the p95 speed near 0.42 viewport units per second, but that number is partly assumed. Trails persist at 0.965 per frame, which halves a bright level in about 20 frames (roughly 0.67 s at 30 fps). Faint residue stalls at a stored level of about 14/255 and never fully clears. Content therefore leaves several visible generations of the wave drifting inward, with older ones fading within a second or so. The wave line itself reshapes with the audio waveform.

Brightness and contrast. The calibrated mean luminance is about 0.23 (interval 0.04 to 0.46), a mid-dark to medium picture. A composite gamma of up to 2x brightens the image before clamping, and the 0.5 echo overlays brighten it a little more. The "darken centre" diamond border removes up to 3/32 of the level per frame at the centre. That makes the centre a little dimmer than the periphery, which is where fed-back brightness accumulates. Contrast is probably moderate to high between the full-strength wave line and a darker or greyer background. If the large polygon dominates, the overall level could be much higher than 0.23, and that case sits inside the wide interval.

Audio response. The wave shape follows the waveform continuously. Bass drives the wave's r, g and b continuously, and bass, mid and treble all drive the wave_mystery term (a position/shape offset) and the polygon's rotation, all with threshold or clamp behaviour. No linear gains are exported, so I cannot say how strong these responses are. Expect the wave line to wobble, change shape and shift colour with the music, and the polygon to twist or rotate with the beat. A random redraw feeds q1, q2 and wave_mystery, so some shape and position changes are random draws at unknown times.

Flashing and strobing. I expect no strobe. The source has no audio switch site (discontinuities 0 Hz, although absence is not proof). The estimated count of global brightness jumps is about 0.08 per 10 s (interval 0 to about 3 per 10 s). The assumed coherent bass-driven colour changes are small (peak delta about 0.03, up to 0.3 in the wide assumed range, at roughly 0.8 to 4 Hz). Bass-coupled colour and brightness pulsing of the wave is plausible and may be noticeable on strong kick drums, but whole-screen flashing is not supported by the source. The upper end of the interval leaves a small chance of occasional brightness jumps if the large white polygon changes with the audio. The calibrated activity percentile is about 0.26 (interval 0.005 to 0.77). That falls between Chill and Normal, with Intense possible only at the top of the interval.

Uncertainty. Confidence is low. Unknown or unresolved in the recipe: the exact palette and hue range reached, mean luma, how visible the clipped polygon is, whether it creates a large white field, the strength of every audio response, the timing of the random redraws, and the true density of the trail generations. Much of the motion statistics are assumed rather than derived, and the screen-level numbers come from a calibrated estimate, not from a render of this preset.
