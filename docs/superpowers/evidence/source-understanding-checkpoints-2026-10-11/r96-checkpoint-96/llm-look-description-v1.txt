LLM look description from the SDK recipe, source parts only (before capture, r96 v1; the recipe's engine-measured facets block was deliberately not used):

Core picture. Roughly five seconds in, I expect a full-screen, busy, organic-looking field rather than a dark background with a few clean objects. The recipe says the previous frame is resampled every frame through a warp shader that steers the image with its own content and with brightness gradients (mechanisms image_driven_advection and gradient_driven_advection, both in the warp stage). That gives smeared, flowing, fluid-like structure. On top of that, two groups of polygons are stamped in: a 14-sided polygon repeated 512 times and a 23-sided polygon repeated 1024 times. These are many small instanced shapes, not tracked particles, and their positions and radii are driven by audio. I expect a dense scatter or swarm of polygons that the feedback smears into streaks and blobs. A built-in waveform (mode 7, two parallel traces, additive, not thick, not dots) is also drawn. Its authored alpha is a constant 0.001, so it is probably almost invisible unless the engine's mode-specific alpha boost lifts it. I would not count on seeing clear waveform lines. The composite stage samples through a reciprocal-radius polar mapping plus noise-driven coordinate displacement. That is a tunnel-like or radially warped look with shimmer, but the recipe marks the tunnel appearance as not guaranteed. Radius and instance positions are unknown, so I cannot say where the polygons cluster or how large they are.

Colour. The wave's raw colour cycles, with each channel between 0.6 and 1.1 (r period 14.4 s, g 11.5 s, b 8.37 s). Over 120 s that visits green 22%, pink 15%, blue 14%, grayscale 10%, yellow 10%, red 10%, cyan 9%, orange 6% and purple 5%. That describes the wave's raw colour, not what is displayed, and the wave is nearly transparent. The shape, warp and composite palettes are all marked unresolved. The one source-derived steady-state cue is an orange hue family, with display colour about (0.50, 0.48, 0.43), a warm, slightly desaturated tan or orange-grey base. The calibrated estimates are mean saturation about 0.59 (interval 0.31 to 0.86) and a warm/cool value of 0.08 (interval -0.26 to 0.43). I read that as moderately saturated and roughly neutral between warm and cool, leaning slightly warm, with a mixed multi-hue palette that shifts over time. The composite colours also depend on bass, mid and treble, so tints move with the music. The recipe does not say which hue dominates.

Motion. The mesh field is mostly a slow rotation. Rotation oscillates between -0.01 and +0.01 rad per frame, and the rotation and zoom centre wanders inside roughly 0.29 to 0.71 in x and y, so the swirl centre drifts around the middle of the screen. Zoom is about 1.009 per frame plus a mid-band term, which gives a slight continuous radial expansion or contraction. The sampling convention is source = centre + (p - centre)/zoom, so I read zoom above 1 as content spreading outward. The warp shader adds small offsets of up to about 0.009 in x and 0.017 in y of the texture per frame. Trails are short: stored feedback gain is 0.80 with a +0.06 offset, so the half-life is about 3 frames (0.10 s). The long smear therefore comes from advection, not from slow fade. The calibrated estimate for mean flow is about 0.31 screen widths per second (interval 0.15 to 0.60), and mean frame difference is about 0.084 (interval 0.044 to 0.145). So I expect fast, restless churn. The shapes' own motion is audio-driven and unknown in speed, and I would not claim a specific path.

Brightness and contrast. The calibrated mean luminance is about 0.38 (interval 0.19 to 0.62), so mid-brightness and quite uncertain. The composite's flat-canvas transfer is unusual. A black canvas maps to near white (about 0.999, 0.996, 0.981), mid-grey 0.5 maps to about 0.49, 0.47, 0.42, and full white maps to about 0.08, so the output looks roughly inverted. It is also time-varying, with large channel spread at the mid and high levels. The source flat-field settles to grey 0.29 in about 0.5 s. That suggests bright, light-toned empty areas with dark edges and drawn content, but the recipe warns that flat transfer does not describe a canvas full of texture, and the steady-state estimate (about 0.5) disagrees with a pure reading of the transfer table. So I expect mid-to-light overall with strong local contrast and cannot say whether the background is mostly pale or mid-tone. The estimate gives about 2.3 global brightness jumps per 10 s (interval 0.09 to 11.2), so occasional pulses are possible and strong flashing is not ruled out.

Audio response. Bass, mid and treble all feed the composite's red, green and blue (threshold or clamped, no linear gains given), so the overall tint and brightness should shift with the music. Both shape groups have their opacity driven by bass, mid and treble, and their positions are driven by bass, treble and mid (shape_0 by bass and treble, shape_1 by bass and mid). The routes are threshold-style, so I expect polygon visibility and placement to jump around on hits. Radius has a continuous response from bass and treble with small gains (0.010 for the 14-gon, 0.0133 for the 23-gon). The wave's position follows attenuated bass, and zoom follows attenuated mid, a smooth breathing. Overall activity is estimated at 0.82 (Intense, with Normal also possible), and the approximate mood is Psychedelic with an Organic / Liquid tag at medium confidence. The strict source model gives no eligible mood group. None of these routes establishes visible strength or beat synchronisation.

Uncertainty. The recipe has no engine-measured facets, and its numbers are source-calibrated estimates with wide intervals. Unknown or unresolved: shape size, colours and where the shapes sit on screen. Also unknown: how visible the waveform is at alpha 0.001, the displayed palette and dominant hue, whether the composite really looks like a tunnel, whether the background is pale or mid-tone, how strongly the audio routes show up, and the actual speed of the polygons. The recipe's own warning applies: this is an approximate description, not a pixel-exact specification.
