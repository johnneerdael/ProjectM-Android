LLM look description from the SDK recipe, source parts only (before capture, r76 v1; the recipe's engine-measured facets block was deliberately not used):

Core picture: this looks like a full-screen, organic, fluid-like feedback pattern rather than a drawn object. Most of the canvas should be patterned. The warp stage adds a finer copy of the fed-back image and subtracts a coarser blur every frame (pattern growth), so small seeds grow into blobs, cells or splatter that spread across the screen. On top of that the image steers its own sampling (self-advection), flows along its own gradients (reaction-diffusion or liquid look), and is pushed by a noise texture. The composite then shows the canvas through a four-direction relief stage, so edges and texture read like embossed or lit relief, with a periodic wobble or ripple of small displacement. The only drawn element is one 100-sided polygon (effectively a circle) at screen centre with a large radius of about 0.79. It is stationary, and its fill is cyan-ish to white. It is a seed for the feedback, not a clear ring. How visible it is on screen is unverified.

Colour: the recipe does not resolve the shader palettes, so the colours depend on the fed-back content. The settled-canvas cue points to a yellow hue family, roughly yellow-green to olive. The composite flat-colour ramp is warm: dark areas stay black, mid-dark areas are orange-brown, and bright areas move toward cream and white. The blue channel of that ramp shifts a little over time, so the tint drifts slowly. Calibrated estimates give moderate-to-fairly-high saturation (about 0.62, range 0.34 to 0.89) and a slightly warm lean (about +0.13, range from slightly cool to clearly warm). Treat these as estimates, not measurements. Monochrome or pastel cannot be ruled in or out.

Motion and feedback: trails are long. The flat-content warp gain is about 0.999 in red, 0.98 in green and 1.0 in blue, so content is not faded quickly and blue persists fully. Green settles lower, so the canvas stays lit instead of fading to black. The calibrated flow estimate is about 0.13 screen widths per second (range 0.04 to 0.29), with a mean frame-to-frame change of about 0.035. That is gentle-to-moderate drifting, smeared motion, not fast whirling. The nominal zoom is 1, so there is no tunnel. Rotation, translation and scale are audio-driven or random, and their sizes are unresolved. Per-pixel sampling offsets are small, at most about 0.7 to 1.25 percent of the screen in the warp and about 2 to 4 percent in the composite. The structure is mostly liquid and organic, with weak symmetry and no sign of fractal or kaleidoscope construction.

Audio: bass, mid and treble all feed the red, green and blue colour of the composite and the green of the warp. They also drive the feedback's rotation, translation and scale (bass also scales x, and bass and mid scale y). Everything is routed through thresholds or clamps, so the response is stepwise, not smooth. There is no audio gain I can state. The visible strength and any beat sync are unresolved. The pack-relative activity is Normal (about 0.35, wide interval spanning Chill to Intense). The mood layer's own guess leans Intense, but that rests on assumed rates. Brightness jumps are about 1 per 10 seconds (0 to about 7), so some pulsing is possible but not certain. I would not call it a strobe.

Brightness: the calibrated mean luminance is about 0.29 (range 0.10 to 0.53), so it is mid-dark overall and could be dim to fairly bright. The composite's relief stage and fine texture can make it look brighter than the flat-colour ramp suggests.

Random and chaotic: the preset redraws q21 to q30 and the rotation value with its random generator during play. Any colours, offsets or motion that read these values are a random draw that changes over time. The recipe gives no schedule for the redraws. The warp and composite also depend on noise-texture and self-advection feedback, so the exact pattern is chaotic. Two loads or runs will look different in detail but share the same liquid, embossed, yellowish-warm character. Displacement and rotation also depend on unresolved persistent-state values.
