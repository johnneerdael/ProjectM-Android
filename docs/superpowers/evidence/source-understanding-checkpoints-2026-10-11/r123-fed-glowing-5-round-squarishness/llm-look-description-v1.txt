LLM look description from the SDK recipe, source parts only (before capture, r123 v1, core recipe view; the recipe's engine-measured facets block was deliberately not used):

Core picture. After about 5 seconds I expect a mostly dark, soft, hazy field with a cyan-leaning cast, with small geometric shapes drawn on top and smeared by feedback into glowing trails and haze. The drawn content is three polygon groups: a 100-sided shape (effectively a circle) with 7 instances, a 32-sided shape with 33 instances, and a very small triangle (NDC radius 0.01) with 311 instances. The triangles rotate through 0 to 6 rad on a roughly 18 s sine cycle. They are tiny, so they read as a scatter of fine specks, not as large solid shapes. The shape centres are not resolved from source. I cannot say whether the 33 and 311 instances form a ring, a grid or a random spread. The circle group's own fill is black (r=g=b=0, alpha 1), so it may cut dark discs into the feedback as much as add light. I would not describe a fractal or a mirrored kaleidoscope: the mirror/symmetry score is 0.0 and no fold operators were found. The structure is geometric overall (geometric score 0.85, organic 0.42).

Feedback and trails. The feedback runs through a custom warp shader with a 12-tap neighbourhood blur at offsets 3 and 6. Flat content keeps about 0.737 of its level per frame, with a half-life of about 2.3 frames (about 0.076 s). That is a short trail. Anything drawn blurs and fades within a few frames, and the steady-state level of a full-brightness input is only about 2.6%. The blur is fixed: it does not depend on position, audio or time. A mesh zoom of 0.975 to 1.0001 (effective zoom 0.967 to 1.0002, zoomexp 0.757) pulls the image toward the centre by up to 3.3% per frame, depending on bass and treble, and pushes it out by at most 0.02%. Rotation and translation are zero. So the motion is a breathing, inward-pulling flow with no sideways drift and no spin from the warp. The triangles' own rotation and the audio-driven shape positions add movement on top. The outer 1% border is blended to black each frame, so a thin dark frame is likely at the edges.

Composite look. The final composite adds noise-driven displacement of the sampling coordinates, with a UV offset of about 0.07 in one stage. It also adds spatially oscillating bands in the radius direction, a cosine of a radial phase with a time-dependent term. Expect a shimmering, wavy or ring-banded distortion of the haze, not a clean picture. The composite maps flat canvas levels to grey: 0.25 maps to about 0.28, 0.5 to 0.35, 0.75 to 0.52 and 1.0 to 0.85. The map is the same for R, G and B, and its spread over time is large (up to about 0.48). So the picture is brightened and its contrast is non-linear, and it changes with time. The source steady state is a very dark cyan (display RGB about 0.017, 0.019, 0.019), which is a lower bound.

Colour. The source palette is mostly unresolved. The two shapes with a known palette are possible grayscale. The shader colour comes from sampled feedback, not from authored constants. The steady-state hue family is cyan, and the estimated warm/cool balance is near neutral (0.017, interval -0.32 to 0.37). Saturation is estimated at about 0.43 (interval 0.15 to 0.70). Shape 2 has colour and opacity terms that depend on bass, mid and treble, so its colour is audio-driven and may vary a lot. I would describe the palette as a low-saturation cyan-to-teal haze with possible audio-tinted coloured specks, and I would not name a single dominant hue.

Brightness and contrast. The calibrated estimate for mean luminance is about 0.24, in a wide interval of 0.055 to 0.48. That puts it between dark and mid. It is not bright overall and not near-black. The source steady state is very dark, but the composite brightens it. Contrast should be moderate to high locally, with small light specks against a dark hazy ground, and soft globally because of the blur.

Motion. The estimated mean flow is about 0.16 screen widths per second (interval 0.06 to 0.35). Mean frame-to-frame difference is about 0.061 (interval 0.026 to 0.113). The zoom pulls things toward the centre, and the short half-life means trails do not build up. I expect moderate, restless activity: constant small movement and shimmer, not a slow drift. The activity estimate is 0.71, with possible bands Normal or Intense, and the interval is wide (0.17 to 0.97).

Audio response. Bass and treble drive the zoom, but the response is thresholded or clamped, so the inward pull probably comes in bursts with the music. Bass, mid and treble drive the radius of the 100-sided and 32-sided shapes, so those pulse in size. The attenuated bass moves the 32-sided shape's position. Bass and treble move the position of the tiny triangles, which are clamped, so the specks are likely to jump or shift with kicks and hi-hats. Shape 2's opacity and RGB react to all three bands. No gain values are known, so the strength of any of this on screen is unresolved.

Flashing and strobing. This is the largest uncertainty, and I cannot rule a flicker out. The recipe gives no certain whole-screen flash mechanism. But the calibrated estimate of global brightness jumps is about 16 per 10 s (interval 4.7 to 62), which is 1.6 per second on average. It could be much more frequent. The assumed coherent transition rate is 1.6 Hz (interval 1.6 to 4.0), with an assumed peak change of 0.06 to 0.3. Colour and opacity changes that follow the bands, together with the thresholded zoom and the time-varying composite, could produce visible brightness pulses with the beat. A fast or hard strobe is not supported by the source facts, but I would not call it safe for flash-sensitive viewers. I expect pulsing and shimmer in time with the music, more than discrete flashes.

Uncertainty. Shape positions, colours and the gains of the audio routes are not resolved in the recipe. Random-texture and per-load randomness are absent (no random redraws, no random textures). The mood estimates are low confidence. The approximate scores are intensity about 38 (interval 31 to 100), psychedelic about 43, geometric as the one supported tag, and the profile fits are best for pop, jazz and chillout. The real picture could be darker and sparser than I describe if the specks fade faster than they are redrawn. It could also be brighter and busier if shape 2 is large and bright. The 5-second state depends on the audio actually playing.
