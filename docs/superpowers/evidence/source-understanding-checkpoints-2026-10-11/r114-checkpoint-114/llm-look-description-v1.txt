LLM look description from the SDK recipe, source parts only (before capture, r114 v1; the recipe's engine-measured facets block was deliberately not used):

Core picture. About five seconds in, I expect a dim, soft, bluish-violet field filled by a swirling feedback image, not a crisp drawing on black. The picture is carried by the feedback warp: a per-pixel warp makes the image swirl around the screen centre with a radius-dependent twist, while an angle-dependent zoom ripple (nine lobes around the centre, sin(9*ang), slowly modulated in time) pulls it in and out. Rotation per step runs from about -0.06 to +0.06 rad and changes sign with radius, so inner and outer regions counter-rotate and shear. The centre of the stretch/rotation moves slowly around the middle of the screen (cx and cy swing roughly 0.5 +/- 1.3 in a cosine of radius and time). The warp shader also uses image-driven advection (it reads the blurred and main images), and the composite shader is a periodic wobble of the sampling coordinates. Nothing translates the whole picture (dx = dy = 0). On top of this sit four large translucent polygons: shape_0 is a white 33-sided disc centred at (0.5, 0.5) with radius about 0.72 (NDC), opaque in the middle and fading to transparent at its rim, so it reads as a soft white glow in the middle of the screen. Shapes 1-3 are 34-sided polygons of radius about 0.88 filled blue (0,0,0.5), green (0,0.5,0) and red (0.5,0,0) at alpha about 0.16, fading to transparent at the rim. Their centres move, but the recipe does not resolve where. They overlap into broad, faint coloured washes, not distinct objects. Two custom point-cloud waves (512 points each, grayscale, alpha about 0.044) sit mainly along or just below the bottom edge (y about 0.79-1.21, x within about +/-0.16 of the centre line), so they are mostly faint and partly off screen. The built-in wave (two parallel waveform traces, additive) has a constant alpha of 0.001, so it is effectively invisible.

Colour. There is no single fixed palette. The feedback decays channel by channel: red keeps about 0.67 of its level per frame, green about 0.53 and blue about 0.76. Anything bright therefore fades toward blue and violet, and green disappears fastest. The recipe's steady-state estimate for the settled canvas is a dim blue-violet of about RGB (0.14, 0.14, 0.19), hue family blue. The coloured shapes feed this with blue, green and red tints, so I expect a cool purple-blue haze with occasional warmer or greener patches near the shapes' overlaps. The built-in wave colour cycles over all hues (periods of about 5.6, 5.1 and 4.7 s), but its alpha is negligible, so that cycling should not show as clear colour. The composite shader leaves flat levels unchanged (a grey ramp), so it adds no tint of its own. The recipe's calibrated estimate gives mean saturation of about 0.56 (range 0.29-0.83) and a slightly cool balance (warm/cool about -0.23, range -0.56 to +0.13). The white centre glow should look pale blue-white once the feedback has tinted it.

Motion. Movement is continuous swirling and breathing of the feedback pattern, not travelling objects. Trails are short: the warp transfer gives a half-life of only 2-3 frames (about 0.1 s), so the image has little memory, and contrast is soft. The swirl and nine-fold angular ripple make a gently kaleidoscopic or twisting texture around the centre. The centre of the twist drifts, so the pattern slowly wanders. The recipe's calibrated mean flow is about 0.08 screen widths per second (range 0.006-0.20), so this is mostly slow to moderate drift and not fast motion. The mean frame-to-frame difference is about 0.026 (range 0-0.067). The shape centres move, but their speeds are unknown.

Brightness and contrast. Overall dim to medium-dim. The calibrated mean luminance is about 0.20, but its interval is wide (0.01-0.44), so I do not know how dark it is. Contrast should be low to moderate: a brighter whitish disc in the middle on a murky blue-violet background with fine feedback texture, and no hard black or white extremes except where the white shape is strongest. The calibrated activity percentile is about 0.14 (interval 0.001-0.70), and its primary band is Chill, with Normal and Intense not ruled out. I expect no strobing: the estimated brightness jumps are about 0.6 per 10 s (range 0-5), and the source model shows no flashing route. This is an estimate, not a proof.

Audio response. The recipe lists no audio bindings and no audio-driven brightness route. The only audio-reading elements are the built-in waveform traces (effectively invisible at alpha 0.001) and the custom waves, which are the faint grayscale point clouds along the bottom (their alpha is a function of an input called value1, capped at 1, and the recipe does not say how value1 relates to audio). So I would expect the visible picture to look almost the same with or without music. The warp, shape and centre-drift controls are time-driven in the recipe, so the motion should not follow the beat. Any beat reaction would come only from the faint bottom point clouds and from whatever the unresolved shape-centre or warp-shader inputs read, and the recipe does not establish either.

Uncertainty. The recipe is an approximate source description, not a render. Unknowns that matter most: where shapes 1-3 sit and how they move (their x and y are unresolved), so the layout of the coloured washes is a guess; whether shape_0 is textured (a texture zoom is listed, but no verified bound image); what the image-driven advection in the warp shader does to the look in detail (it may add blur-like or splatter-like texture, but this is not established); the true overall brightness (the interval is very wide); how much of the faint grayscale wave content falls on screen (it sits at or beyond the bottom edge); and whether the haze looks smooth or textured. The colour statements come from source gains and a settled-state estimate, not from observed frames. The recipe's mood and activity numbers are editable, uncalibrated assumptions plus a pack-relative display estimate, and the Chill band is the estimate, not a certain result.
