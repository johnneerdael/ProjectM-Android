LLM look description from the SDK recipe, source parts only (before capture, r98 v1; the recipe's engine-measured facets block was deliberately not used):

Core picture. About five seconds in, I expect a single wave line drawn as a closed, audio-deformed circle centred on the screen (wave_x and wave_y are both 0.5). It is a thin, non-dotted, non-thick line with ordinary alpha blending (builtin_wave: audio_deformed_circle, mode 0, source_alpha_over). The ring is about 0.5 model units in radius. Bass shifts that radius a little through wave_mystery, with a gain of only 0.1. The waveform (the right channel) pushes the line in and out with a sample radial gain of 0.4, so the circle is wobbly or jagged rather than a clean ring. The ring nominally rotates at about 0.2 rad/s. Behind and around it, the previous frames are fed back through a radial warp (mesh_warp: radial feedback transform plus radial twist). That smears the ring into soft concentric echoes and swirling, spiral-like trails, with the zoom and rotation changing with distance from the centre. This should read as a glowing wobbly ring that bleeds into a slowly swirling tunnel. It is not a set of many separate objects, and the recipe lists no shapes, particles, mirrors or tiling (mirror symmetry score 0, only a weak mechanism-based 0.6 symmetry estimate). The recipe does not say how far the swirl spreads across the screen.

Colour. The ring is max-normalised: its RGB is divided by its largest channel, so the line is drawn at full strength in whatever hue the three channels give. That makes it a vivid, saturated line and not a dim grey one. Red follows bass, green follows treble and blue follows mid, each with a gain of 0.4 around a base of about 0.4. The hue therefore shifts continuously with the music's band balance, and the recipe marks the colour as cycling. I cannot name one fixed hue. Bass-heavy passages should lean red, treble-heavy passages green, and mid-heavy passages blue. The recipe gives no palette values (hue variety, coloured support and hue rate are all null). The settled feedback canvas is grayscale and very dark (display about 0.0036, a lower bound), so the trails mostly inherit the ring's recent colours and fade, rather than sitting on a coloured background. The calibrated estimate puts mean saturation at about 0.32 (10th to 90th percentile 0.04 to 0.58) and warm/cool near neutral at +0.06 (-0.28 to +0.41). So the overall impression is moderate colour with no clear warm or cool bias, and the line is brighter and more saturated than its fading trails.

Motion. The main motion is the swirl and zoom of the feedback, with no sideways drift: dx and dy are both 0, scale is 1 and cx/cy are 0.5. Zoom varies with radius, and rotation is a radius-dependent twist, so content spirals around the centre. The zoom and rotation formulas depend on slow counters (colorcounter, counter1, counter2, cdelay2), so the swirl direction or amount probably steps through a few phases over time. I cannot say which phase is active at 5 s. Bass and treble also nudge the zoom, but the recipe marks that as thresholded or clamped, with no linear gain given. The calibrated estimate gives a mean optical flow of about 0.12 screen widths per second (0.03 to 0.27), mean frame-to-frame difference of about 0.03 (0.002 to 0.073) and few global brightness jumps (about 0.8 per 10 s, range 0 to 5.8). That suggests a smooth, moderately flowing picture and not a violent one. The trails fade with a decay of 0.97 per frame, a half-life of about 0.78 s, and a faint residue floor at stored level 16/255 stays behind. Old ring positions therefore linger for roughly a second or two as echoes and then thin out, but never fully black out.

Brightness and contrast. The calibrated mean luminance is about 0.22, with a wide interval of 0.03 to 0.46. I expect a mostly dark-to-mid frame with a bright coloured line and fading echoes, so contrast between the line and the background should be fairly high. The legacy composite applies a gamma weight of 2, which brightens the image up to 2x before clamping, so the ring and its first few echoes should look punchy and not dull. Echo is inactive. The source-only steady state is a lower bound and is unreliable for brightness, so I defer to the calibrated estimate. The calibrated activity percentile is about 0.25 (band Chill, but the interval 0.004 to 0.79 allows Normal or Intense). The recipe's own approximate mood says smooth (about 95) and low intensity (about 10, with a wide interval of 4 to 100), with tags Smooth and Hypnotic, at low confidence.

Audio response. The waveform shapes the circle, so the outline visibly jitters with the sound. Bass raises alpha (gain 1.0), brightens red (0.4) and slightly enlarges the ring (0.1). Mid brightens blue (0.4) and treble brightens green (0.4), so the line's hue and strength follow the spectrum continuously rather than flashing at beats. Bass and treble also modulate the zoom of the feedback through thresholded terms, which may add a pulse or breathing in the swirl, but the strength is unknown. The recipe assumes coherent flash transitions of roughly 0.8 to 4 Hz, with small deltas of about 0.03 to 0.3. I treat those as assumptions, not as measured strobing, and I expect no whole-screen flashing.

Uncertainty. This is a source-only, approximate recipe. Palette, saturation, hue range and mean luma are not resolved from the source, and the numbers above come from the calibrated display estimate and its wide intervals. The exact ring size on screen, how much of the screen the echoes cover, the swirl phase at 5 s, the direction of rotation, and how strongly bass and treble pulse the zoom are all unknown. The zoom and rotation depend on unresolved counter states. Native rounding and the drawing and feedback interaction could make real frames look different, for example more filled or less filled than I describe. Mood and activity values are editable research assumptions and not calibrated viewer data. I did not use rendered images.
