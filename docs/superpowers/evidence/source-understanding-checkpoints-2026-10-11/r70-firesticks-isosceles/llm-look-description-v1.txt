LLM look description from the SDK recipe, source parts only (before capture, r70 v1; the recipe's engine-measured facets block was deliberately not used):

Core picture: two custom point-cloud waves (wave_0 and wave_1, 512 points each per frame) draw swirling, looping strands of dots whose positions are closed-form functions of time and sample index (sums of sines and cosines with a "flip" state that alternates a 2x-rate term). They occupy a region left of and slightly above the middle of the frame (wave_0 roughly x 0.16-0.65, y 0.08-0.79; wave_1 roughly x 0.20-0.71, y 0.14-0.78). The two waves are near-twins, offset slightly, so expect a doubled, interlaced set of glowing dotted lines that curl and drift as time passes rather than a filled shape. A built-in dot waveform (the stereo X/Y trace at screen centre) is also present but its authored alpha is 0.001, so it should be essentially invisible. Whether the strands read as thin "fire sticks" or loose beaded loops depends on how the dots join up under feedback; that is unverified.

Colour: each wave's colour is an RGB sine cycle driven by time, so the hue wanders through green, orange, blue, cyan, pink and purple (recipe shares about 21% green, 21% orange, 21% blue, 14% cyan, 14% pink, 7% purple), mean saturation about 0.91. The points fade in along the line: alpha is the square of the sample position, mean about 0.33, so one end of each strand is faint and the other end is bright. Everything is then shown through the composite shader, which acts as a fire-like tone ramp: a flat canvas level of 0.25 maps to a dull orange (about 0.47, 0.25, 0.11), 0.5 to yellow, and 0.75 and above to white. The source hints the settled overall tint is orange (hue_family orange), so I expect bright overlaps to burn orange to yellow to white, with the original multi-hue dots mostly showing where content is dim. This ramp is time-invariant. The exact hues actually reached on screen are not verified, because the warp and composite palettes are marked unresolved.

Feedback and motion: the previous frame is multiplied by roughly 0.912 per frame (plus a small 0.008 offset), a half-life of about 7 frames, roughly 0.23 s at 30 fps. Trails are therefore short; dim content collapses quickly and settles near a low floor (about 0.018 of full) instead of persisting, while bright content is kept better (retention about 0.92 at full level, 0.59 at a quarter level). There is no zoom, rotation or translation (zoom 1, rot 0, dx and dy 0). The only transport is the warp shader, which displaces sampling by a noise texture (a very small offset, around 0.0006 to 0.001 in UV) so the image shimmers and smears slightly with a turbulent, organic texture rather than flowing in one direction. The mirror/symmetry estimate is zero, so no kaleidoscope or tunnel look. Expect gentle, smooth motion: the calibrated estimate gives mean flow about 0.07 screen widths per second (interval 0.001 to 0.18) and frame-to-frame difference about 0.026.

Audio: the recipe lists no audio bindings. Only the nearly invisible built-in waveform follows the left/right waveform. The custom waves are computed from time and sample index (audio held at midpoints), with no closed-form audio-driven brightness. I would expect little or no visible beat reaction, no bass pulse and no strobe: the flash estimates are 0, and the source model has no audio brightness route. Any music response would be indirect and not established by the recipe.

Brightness and mood: the picture is likely fairly dark overall, mostly black with a modest tone of dotted bright lines. Calibrated mean luminance is about 0.14 (10th to 90th percentile 0 to 0.37), activity 0.13 (Chill, possibly Normal), global brightness jumps about 0.15 per 10 s (up to about 3). Saturation is estimated at about 0.34 (0.06 to 0.61) after the tone ramp, with a warm lean (warm_cool 0.55; the source-only estimate is 0.17, so warm versus cool is uncertain). Approximate tags: Smooth, Organic / Liquid; suited to ambient, chillout, jazz and classical profiles. Uncertain or open: the real feedback-driven look, because the warp and composite are noise- and texture-dependent. Random or chaotic elements: the recipe lists no per-load random branches (random_redraws is null), so nothing is declared random per load. The noise-driven warp and the persistent "flip" state may make the exact pattern of the strands vary from run to run; the recipe does not quantify this.
