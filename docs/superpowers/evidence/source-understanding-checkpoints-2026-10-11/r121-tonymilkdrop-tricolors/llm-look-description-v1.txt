LLM look description from the SDK recipe, source parts only (before capture, r121 v1, core recipe view; the recipe's engine-measured facets block was deliberately not used):

Approximate look after about 5 seconds: a dense, organic, fluid-looking feedback pattern, not a clean drawing on black. The only thing the preset draws is one thick, closed circular line (an audio-deformed ring) centred on the screen. Its radius is small, about 0.2 model units (builtin_wave.effective_base_radius_model_units = 0.19999998807907104), and the waveform pushes it in and out (sample_radial_gain 0.4), so it reads as a wobbly ring. The ring turns slowly, nominally 0.2 rad/s. Everything else on screen is that ring's history being fed back, stretched and sharpened every frame.

Core picture. Four feedback mechanisms act on the canvas. (1) The whole image is zoomed outward from the screen centre every frame: zoom 1.03 with zoomexp 13.67, giving an effective zoom from about 1.002 near the centre to about 2.39 at the far edge (0.2% to 139% per step). The centre is nearly still and the edges are flung outward very fast, so the ring's trail becomes a tunnel-like or radial smear that streams outward and fades. There is no rotation and no sideways drift. (2) The warp shader adds a fine copy of the fed-back image and subtracts a coarser blur each frame (blur_difference_feedback). That band-pass sharpening makes small seeds grow into blobs, cells, splatter or vein-like edge patterns over most of the canvas. (3) The warp also steers its own sampling by the image's gradients and by a noise lookup (self-advection, reaction-diffusion-like flow), with sampling offsets of up to about 0.076 horizontally and 0.135 vertically in UV. That smears and swirls the pattern like liquid. (4) The sampling coordinates are tiled with mirrored repeats, so expect repeated, mirrored, kaleidoscope-like copies of the pattern. The recipe's symmetry score is 0, so the mirroring is probably not a clean mirror image, and the pattern is probably only loosely symmetric. The composite then shows the picture as embossed relief (two-direction gradient relief), so edges and texture stand out like lit, carved surfaces and flat areas collapse to one level.

Colour. The ring colour cycles smoothly over time. The red, green and blue channels each follow a slow sine with periods of about 11.1 s, 10.2 s and 9.4 s, so in 5 seconds the hue drifts through roughly half a cycle. The channels are clamped and then divided by the largest one, so the ring is always at full brightness in its strongest channel and the hue shifts through blue, green, red, pink, orange, cyan, yellow and purple. The recipe's 120 s closed-form tally puts blue and green most often at roughly a quarter each, red at 17%, and the rest smaller. The estimated saturation of the ring itself is high (about 0.77). This is the ring's source colour only. The final palette depends on what the feedback shaders do to it, and that is unresolved. The warp is channel-selective on flat content. Red persists with gain 0.966 (halving in about 15 frames). Green is almost never faded (gain 0.993, steady level 1.0). Blue is wiped almost completely each frame (gain 0.045). So the trails and the background tend to go green with some red, with little blue memory, and the steady-state canvas is green (canvas_local_rgb about 0.004, 0.996, 0.022). The composite then tints the whole screen with four slowly cycling corner shades (periods 14.6 s, 19.6 s and 16.2 s, each channel scaled between 0.67 and 1.0). Which hue appears in which corner is random per load, so I cannot name a fixed tint. The composite's flat-canvas response is dim and slightly blue-leaning (full-level white maps to about 0.59, 0.61, 0.70), which dims everything. The steady-state display estimate says "blue" hue family at a very low level. I take that to mean the mid-tones are muted and dim-ish, with the vivid colour mostly in the ring and its fresh trail. Overall I expect a multicolour, shifting palette with green and blue-green tones likely, not a single colour.

Brightness and contrast. This is unclear and my confidence is low. The calibrated display estimate gives a mean luminance of about 0.24 on a 0-1 scale, with a wide 10th to 90th percentile range of 0.06 to 0.48. That is a medium-dark picture where the bright ring and relief highlights stand out against darker, textured regions. Mean saturation is about 0.38 (range 0.11 to 0.65), so moderately coloured, not neon. The relief stage should give fairly sharp local contrast at edges while flat areas stay dim. The mean frame-to-frame difference is about 0.034 (range 0.005 to 0.078), which is small to moderate.

Motion. The motion is a continuous outward flow from the centre with a slowly turning, waveform-wobbling ring in the middle. The estimated mean flow is about 0.08 screen widths per second (range 0.01 to 0.21). Together with the shader advection this should look like a slowly boiling or flowing pattern, not a fast zoom. The p95 speed is about 6.65 viewport units/s, but that figure is assumed, and I do not read it as the typical speed. The source also bounds how fast sampled content can move, but that is a ceiling and not what you will see. Nothing translates or rotates the whole image. The motion is zoom-outward, shader swirl and ring rotation only. The strict source model gives no firm activity band. The display estimate leans Chill, with an activity of 0.08 in a range of 0.002 to 0.72, so Normal or Intense cannot be ruled out, and the approximate mood layer says Normal or Intense are possible. My reading is that it is calm to moderate in speed but visually busy and textured.

Audio response. The only established audio link is the waveform shape of the ring (waveform_right, continuous, unsmoothed). Louder or more varied audio makes the ring's outline more jagged and wobbly, and that new shape is then smeared into the feedback. The recipe finds no bass, mid or treble route to colour, brightness, zoom or any other parameter (audio_bindings is empty, bass-to-RGB effect 0). Nothing indicates a beat-synchronised pulse. Audio changes what is drawn, not how the whole screen is lit.

Flashing and strobing. No source mechanism for strobing was found. No audio threshold switch was found (discontinuities 0 Hz, but the recipe says absence is not proof). The estimated global brightness jumps are 0 per 10 s, with an upper bound of about 2.4 per 10 s. The colour changes are slow sine cycles of around 9 to 20 s. So the expected behaviour is smooth hue drift without flashing or strobing. This is not a certified no-flash result, because the coherent flash rate and size are listed as unknown and could be up to 4 Hz and 0.3 in the assumed intervals. The ring is also max-normalised, which on silence or very weak colour could flip between full colour and near black, but the colour channels are at roughly +-0.5 and not near the 0.01 threshold for most of the cycle, so I do not expect that to be a visible flicker.

Uncertainty. The mood tags are Psychedelic (score 78) and Organic/Liquid, estimated and uncalibrated. The overall mood confidence is low. I am most unsure about three things. First, the actual brightness and background colour, which come from the unresolved shader feedback. Second, how much of the screen the band-pass sharpening patterns fill, and whether they look like cells, veins or splatter. Third, how strong and how loosely mirrored the tiling is. The recipe states that real frames will differ from this source-based description because of the drawn content, the audio and native rounding.
