LLM look description from the SDK recipe, source parts only (before capture, r115 v1; the recipe's engine-measured facets block was deliberately not used):

Core picture. About 5 seconds in, I expect a soft, smeared, fluid-looking full-screen feedback image rather than crisp shapes. Three custom waveform lines (wave_0, wave_1, wave_2, 512 points each, same colour recipe) are drawn every frame at very low opacity (mean alpha 0.05), so each one only deposits a faint trace. Those traces are fed back through a warp that expands the whole image outward from the screen centre (zoom 1.104, zoomexp 0.109). The expansion is slow near the centre (about 0.6% per step) and much stronger toward the edges (up to about 148% per step), so content streams outward and stretches into radial, tunnel-like or expanding smears. A self-advection warp, where the image steers its own sampling coordinates by about -0.011 to +0.019 UV, adds a flowing, fluid distortion. The composite shows the result through a two-direction gradient/relief stage. Edges and texture should read like embossed or lit relief, and flat areas collapse to one dim level. The line positions follow closed-form functions of sample index, time and band-smoothed audio (bass_att, mid_att, treb_att, vol). The recipe does not give their actual coordinates or screen coverage ("position_bounds" is null, displayed coverage unresolved), so I can't say where on screen the lines sit. There is no mirror or kaleidoscope symmetry (score 0.0), no particles and no fractal construction. The look is organic and liquid, not geometric.

Colour. The waveform colour recipe produces a mix of hues. By share of points: pink about 42%, purple 14%, cyan 10%, red 8%, green 8%, blue 6%, orange 5%, grey 4%, yellow 3%. Mean point colour is roughly (0.82, 0.50, 0.82), a magenta-pink-lilac, with mean saturation about 0.57. So I expect the main impression to be pink and purple with cyan and red accents and a small amount of other hues. The settled flat-canvas colour is purple (display about RGB 0.044, 0.030, 0.050, very dark violet). The composite also multiplies in cycling corner tints. The red, green and blue channels cycle with periods of about 14.6 s, 19.6 s and 16.2 s, each ranging between 2/3 and 1 of full level. The tint placement is random per load, so I can't say which hue appears where, but the tint should drift slowly over tens of seconds. The strict palette summary is only partly resolved: mean saturation 0.37, warm/cool balance nearly neutral (0.05, slightly warm). The shader palettes for the warp and composite are marked unresolved by the recipe.

Motion. The dominant motion is the outward radial expansion from the centre. It is a steady zoom with no rotation (rot 0), no translation (dx = dy = 0) and no per-frame control variation. Feedback sampling is slow near the centre and fast toward the edges. Trails persist for a long time: the warp gain is 0.996 per frame, a half-life of about 172 frames, or about 5.7 s at 30 fps. Drawn marks therefore accumulate and smear outward rather than disappearing quickly, so I expect long flowing streaks and a full, layered canvas. The recipe's own calibrated estimate gives a mean flow of about 0.29 screen widths per second (10th-90th percentile 0.14 to 0.56), moderately fast. Its mean frame-to-frame difference is about 0.10 (0.06 to 0.17). The recipe's source-bound speed figures (about 6.8 viewport units/s) rest on assumed angular rates, so I treat them as unreliable.

Brightness and contrast. The recipe is unsure here. The flat-field model decays to black, and the composite's flat-area level is very dark (about 0.04 to 0.05). That is only the level of empty, flat regions, and the recipe says it does not describe the displayed picture, because the relief stage makes texture-rich canvases look brighter. The recipe's calibrated display estimate gives mean luminance about 0.28 (interval 0.09 to 0.52), so my best guess is a moderately dim-to-medium picture with a dark purple-black background and brighter pink and lilac streaks, with a wide uncertainty range. Mean saturation is estimated at about 0.74 (0.47 to 1.0), so colours are fairly vivid where present. Contrast is likely high locally, because the relief stage emphasises edges.

Audio response. The only explicit audio routes are on the composite: attenuated bass and attenuated treble each feed the red, green and blue colour channels. The gain is not linear, since it is thresholded or clamped, and the recipe gives no linear gain. The estimated peak colour effect is small, about 0.05 per band, with assumed jumps of 0.075 to 0.3. The wave line positions also depend on bass_att, mid_att, treb_att and vol, so the lines should move and swell with the music. The recipe does not resolve how strongly. Because the audio inputs are the smoothed *_att variants, the response should be gradual rather than snappy per-beat. The recipe's estimate of global brightness jumps is large (about 40 per 10 s, interval 13 to 152), but that figure is the calibrated estimate and the source flash numbers are assumed values, so I would not claim strobing. Overall the music probably modulates the overall tint and brightness and the line positions, with no obvious beat-synchronised full-screen pulse.

Uncertainty. The recipe gives no direct evidence of where the lines are drawn, how much of the screen they cover, the real brightness of the composite, or the actual palette produced through the warp and composite shaders. Only the flat-canvas colours and the wave colour shares are known. The mood block gives the activity as Normal, possibly Intense (strict source model: no eligible group), while the calibrated display estimate says Intense. The two disagree, so I put the energy as medium to high. The tags are "Organic / Liquid" and "Reactive". Speed, acceleration, flash rate and symmetry values are marked assumed. The party and classical profile scores are not usable evidence. The hue placement of the corner tints is random per load. Exact line shapes, line count visible at once, and whether the picture reads as tunnel rays, flowing ribbons or a hazy wash are not determined by the recipe.
