LLM look description from the SDK recipe, source parts only (before capture, r126 v1, core recipe view; the recipe's engine-measured facets block was deliberately not used):

Core picture. After about 5 seconds this should read as a soft, smeared, feedback-driven image with a few small flat-shaded polygons drifting over it, not as a crisp geometric scene. Four custom shapes are configured: three five-sided shapes and one triangle, each with a single instance. Two of the pentagons have a nominal radius of about 0.10 in normalised units. That is roughly a tenth of the screen, so they are small. The other pentagon and the triangle have radii the recipe cannot resolve. Shape 1 spins at a constant 2.4 rad/s, a bit under half a turn per second, and wanders across most of the screen: x between about 0.11 and 0.89, y between about 0.14 and 0.86. Its centre speed is at most about 1.36 viewport units per second, which is a ceiling and not a typical speed. Shape 0 has a constant tiny rotation of 0.01 rad. The textured pentagon (shape 2) wanders over roughly the whole width, and the triangle (shape 3) over roughly the whole height. Their speed ceilings are about 0.41 and 0.46 units per second. The recipe lists no built-in or custom waveform and no motion-vector grid. Mirror symmetry is essentially absent (score 0.0 in the mirrors block). Nothing indicates a tunnel, kaleidoscope or fractal. Style looks geometric/feedback-smeared rather than tiled.

Behind the shapes, the previous frame is re-sampled each frame through a warp shader that has a spatially varying zoom, a small constant warp amount of 0.078, and unresolved, audio-linked rotation and translation. Expect the whole image to drift, swirl and breathe slightly, with the shapes leaving soft smeared wakes instead of hard outlines. A texture-driven distortion is possible in the composite, but no random textures are listed, so I cannot say what it looks like.

Trails and persistence. The warp's flat-colour transfer gives a gain of 0.972 per frame with a tiny offset of +0.0012. Trails halve in about 24 frames, or roughly 0.47 s at 30 fps. The steady level for content that starts at full white is only about 0.016. The effect is the same for all three channels (not channel-selective). So trails are visible but short, and old content fades to near black within a couple of seconds. It does not build up into a dense wash.

Colour. The source palette is mostly unresolved: warp, composite and shape feedback palettes are all marked unresolved. What is known: the pentagons and triangle have borders, in a blue/purple tone (about 0.46, 0.37, 0.60) for shape 0, a teal-blue tone (about 0.15, 0.40, 0.48) for shape 1, and pure red (1, 0, 0) for shapes 2 and 3. Shape 2 has white fill (r, g, b = 1) plus a texture request, so it may show a tinted or patterned face. The recipe's steady-state estimate puts the settled canvas in a green hue family with very dark local values (display about 0.037, 0.050, 0.046), a lower bound only. The composite maps a flat canvas through a time-varying tint. For flat input at 2, 5 and 8 seconds, black maps to roughly (0.06, 0.07, 0.06), and a quarter-level input maps to a green-teal (0.19, 0.30, 0.28), with a red spread of about 0.48 across those times. Brighter inputs fall back to very dark values of about 0.02 to 0.05. The composite is therefore non-monotonic: mid-dark content is lifted into teal-green and bright content is pushed down. Expect a dark, muted teal/green field with cyan-blue and purple-ish edges, plus occasional red outlines, and a slowly shifting tint. Estimated mean saturation is moderate-to-high (about 0.5 from the calibrated estimate, 0.69 from the source palette), and the warm/cool balance is neutral (about -0.01 to -0.05, very slightly cool). I cannot say how many hues show at once: the source estimate gives roughly three hue bins, and I treat that as possible hues, not a simultaneous palette.

Brightness and contrast. The calibrated display estimate gives a mean luminance of about 0.23 (10th to 90th percentile 0.04 to 0.47). That is a dim-to-medium image with a very wide range, so it could look quite dark. The composite palette values above (mostly 0.02 to 0.07, with a lifted band near 0.2 to 0.3) point to the darker end, with small bright polygon shapes giving the local contrast. The frame-to-frame change is about 0.057 (interval 0.023 to 0.109), and the flow speed is about 0.26 screen widths per second (0.12 to 0.51). That is lively, medium-fast drift, not a still picture and not frantic.

Motion. Motion comes from three sources: the shapes' wandering paths (ceilings above); the feedback warp, whose rotation and translation depend on bass and whose zoom varies across the screen; and the spinning of shape 1 at 2.4 rad/s. The recipe's source rotation rate is 2.4 rad/s. The warp's own rotation and translation rates are unresolved and tied to bass. Expect a continuously moving, swirling look whose strength rises and falls with the music. The speed numbers in the mood block are assumptions, not measurements.

Audio response. Bass and attenuated bass drive the warp's zoom, rotation and translation in x and y. The recipe records these as clamped or thresholded with unknown gain, so visible strength is unresolved. Bass and attenuated bass also feed the composite's red, green and blue colour channels, again clamped or thresholded with unknown gain. Expect the colour and brightness tint to move with the low end and the image to lurch or shift on bass hits. Two smooth, continuous size links exist. Attenuated mid shrinks shape 2's radius (gain -0.3) and attenuated bass enlarges shape 3's radius (gain +0.22). Both inputs are smoothed, so these are gentle size changes. They are not hard pulses.

Flashing and strobe. This preset does not look like a hard strobe from the source, but the recipe cannot rule out brightness pulsing. The raw bass-to-colour routes in the composite are the only plausible flash mechanism. The calibrated estimate gives about 2 global brightness jumps per 10 seconds, with a very wide interval of 0 to about 10 per 10 seconds. The assumed mood features (not measured) use a coherent transition rate of 4 Hz with a peak delta of 0.15 to 0.30 and a brightness jump of 0.075 to 0.30. Those are placeholders from assumed rules, so I would not read them as expected behaviour. My expectation is a visible bass-linked brightness and tint pulse, mild to moderate in size, on the order of a few per 10 seconds, not a screen-filling strobe. I cannot give a no-strobe guarantee.

Mood and activity. The calibrated activity percentile is 0.67 (interval 0.08 to 0.94), band Normal, with Chill and Intense both still possible. The source-potential tag is Intense, but it is uncalibrated. The approximate-mood intensity score is about 69 and the psychedelic score about 20, so I read it as energetic but not trippy: moderately busy, geometric-feedback, dim-teal in tone.

Uncertainty. The largest unknowns are: the actual palette and its dominant hue (all stage palettes unresolved, and the composite tint varies over time); the real size of shapes 2 and 3; whether the texture on shape 2 is visible; the gain of every bass link; how bright the picture really is (the luminance interval spans nearly an order of magnitude); and whether bass produces a noticeable pulse. Every motion speed given is a nominal ceiling. The mood-block motion, flash and brightness-jump features are assumptions, and the display-estimate statistics are calibrated predictions, not a measurement of this preset.
