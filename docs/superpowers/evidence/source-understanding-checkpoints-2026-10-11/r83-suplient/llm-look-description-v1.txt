LLM look description from the SDK recipe, source parts only (before capture, r83 v1; the recipe's engine-measured facets block was deliberately not used):

Core picture: a circular audio waveform (built-in wave mode 0, not dots) is drawn as a closed ring whose outline is deformed by the live waveform. Behind it the screen is a heavily fed-back, swirling field: every frame the previous image is pulled toward the screen centre (zoom 0.988 with a tiny zoom exponent, so the pull varies strongly with radius, from almost nothing near the edge of the sampled range to about 69% per step in the extreme), twisted by a position-dependent rotation (up to about 0.015 rad per step), and shifted by a moving centre that wanders on a small Lissajous-like path (centre roughly 0.2 to 0.8 across the screen). Content is also smeared by an 8-tap neighbourhood blur whose radius grows towards the edges and changes with the audio, and the composite re-samples the image through a blurred copy of itself (self-advection), so the picture reads as flowing, smeared, fluid-like structure rather than crisp lines. A faint 63 x 45 grid of motion vectors is also drawn into the feedback and adds fine streaks. Borders are black and thin (about 1% of the screen), so they barely matter.

Colour: the source gives no reliable palette. The warp, mesh and composite colour ingredients are all listed as unresolved, and the steady-state hue family is grayscale. The composite maps the canvas through a luminance curve that is not monotonic: a flat black canvas shows as white and a mid-grey canvas shows as black, with a 0.5 grey at the quarter level. So I expect a high-contrast, mostly grey or black-and-white look with inverted-looking regions, possibly with some hue from bass-driven red/green/blue terms in the warp. Saturation is estimated at about 0.43 (wide interval 0.15 to 0.70), and warm versus cool is near neutral. Treat any strong hue as uncertain. I cannot name a dominant colour.

Feedback and motion: trails are short. On flat content the warp keeps about 45% of brightness per frame (half-life about one frame), but dim content around the 0.25 level is kept whole, so the canvas settles near that level instead of fading to black. Long ghost trails are therefore unlikely. The persistence comes mainly from the blur and advection smearing. Motion is estimated as moderately fast: mean flow of about 0.24 screen widths per second (interval 0.11 to 0.48), with strong contraction toward the centre and a slow swirl. Because the centre drifts, the whole pattern should slowly wander. These are estimates calibrated from the source, not measurements of this preset.

Audio: the waveform shape follows the live audio continuously. Treble drives the wave's blue channel, with the response thresholded or clamped, so the ring colour or visibility can flip with treble. Bass feeds the red, green and blue terms in the warp shader. The blur radius also reacts to audio, which can make the smear change erratically from frame to frame. The gains are unknown, so I cannot say how strong the reaction is. The recipe lists a nominal brightness-cycle rate of about 14 Hz in the warp shader and a high estimate of global brightness jumps (about 42 per 10 s, interval 13 to 159). It also tags the preset as Flashing, but displayed flashing is unverified. Expect a lively, possibly flickery picture, and do not assume strobing.

Brightness: estimated mean luminance is about 0.49 (interval 0.30 to 0.72), so mid-bright with a wide spread. Frame-to-frame change is fairly high (about 0.07 mean absolute difference). The overall activity estimate is high (0.87, interval 0.42 to 0.99, band Intense or Normal). The grey steady-state level is only a lower bound.

Random, chaotic or undefined: no per-load random branches, random redraws or random textures are listed, so the look should be the same each load apart from the audio. The warp rotation and translation are spatial expressions of position and time and are not fully resolved. The colour palette and persistence orbit are unresolved, and the exact feedback decay is null. The blur and advection make the fine detail chaotic. Nothing is reported as undefined or non-finite. The details of the final look come from several unresolved ingredients, so this description is a plausible guess rather than a certain picture.
