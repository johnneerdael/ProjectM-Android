Approximate look: a mid-brightness, embossed, tinted picture built around one large pentagon at the centre that copies the previous frame into itself, producing nested, slowly turning pentagons (a droste or tunnel effect). This is a source-based estimate with no rendered frames, so some parts are uncertain.

Main structure: shape_1 is a 5-sided polygon centred exactly on the screen (0.5, 0.5) with a large radius of about 0.40, turning at a steady 0.4 rad/s. It draws the previous frame inside itself, scaled by about 0.905. Each frame copies the last one, so the copies nest recursively and should read as receding pentagons that spiral inward while rotating. The copy is tinted orange at the centre fading toward blue at the edge, with alpha 0.5 at the centre and 0 at the edge. A copy of a dark canvas stays dark, though. A faint 63 by 47 grid of short motion-vector lines (alpha about 0.18, length 0.05) is also drawn into the feedback. Expect a fine, fabric-like texture over the picture. Brightness and colour: the composite shows the image through its local slopes (embossed relief) with gradient and blur-driven smearing, so edges and texture stand out. Flat areas read as one level, with a dim blue floor (black maps to about RGB 0, 0, 0.12). The composite also multiplies by cycling corner shades with periods of 15 to 20 s, and their placement is random on each preset load. Hues therefore shift slowly and differ between loads. Calibrated luminance is about 0.39 (range 0.21-0.61), and Dark and Bright are both unlikely. The pooled palette is blue-led (about 32%), then green and pink, with no dominant hue (blue leads, steady-state hue is blue). Saturation is moderate (about 0.44). Pastel is possible but not established.

Movement is dominated by the rotating nested pentagons, so the flow is moderate, about 0.09 screen widths per second. Trails are short: red, green and blue all retain 0.89 per frame, a half-life of about 6 frames (0.2 s). The composite mirrors left-right, giving partial two-fold symmetry (upper estimate 0.69).

Flashing: the no-strobe probability is only 0.08, so call it flickering and not strobe-free. Estimated global brightness jumps are about 3 per 10 s (range 0.4-14).
