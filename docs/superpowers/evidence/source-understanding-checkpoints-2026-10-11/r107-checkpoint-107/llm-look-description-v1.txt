LLM look description from the SDK recipe, source parts only (before capture, r107 v1; the recipe's engine-measured facets block was deliberately not used):

Core picture. Two custom line waveforms (wave_0 and wave_1, 512 points each, closed-form positions) are drawn into a strongly self-advecting feedback canvas. Each wave is a three-leg zigzag made of roughly vertical strokes: sample thirds 0-1/3, 1/3-2/3 and 2/3-1 each run top to bottom across about the full source height, at source x of about 0.5, then 0.4, then 0.3 or 0.4 (the choice depends on an audio-derived value1, so the legs shift sideways with the music). x is stretched by the aspect ratio, so the legs sit left of the centre line and spread roughly a third of a screen width. wave_1 is the same shape mirrored left to right (x = 1 - x) and driven by value2, so I expect a roughly symmetric pair of angular, glyph-like strokes either side of the centre. On top of that the warp stage feeds the image back through a self-advecting, noise-displaced lookup (the noise texture offsets the sampling coordinates by up to about 0.007-0.0125 UV in one lookup and about 0.03-0.05 UV in a second, in a pick-the-nearest-sample construction). That smears and curls the strokes into fluid, turbulent, organic-looking streaks rather than clean lines. The tags are Organic / Liquid and Reactive. After about 5 s I expect smeared, flowing versions of the zigzag strokes spread over much of the screen, not a few crisp lines on an empty background.

Colour. The waves are coloured by a slow rainbow. Red, green and blue are each 0.5 + 0.5*sin(0.1*time + 3*sample + audio term), with green offset by 60 degrees and blue by 120 degrees. Hue therefore runs along each stroke by about 3 radians over its length (roughly half a hue wheel), and the whole pattern rotates once about every 63 s, which is barely visible at the 5 s mark. The recipe gives no mean hue shares, so the exact hues at 5 s are unresolved. The composite then displays the canvas through a per-channel inverting curve. The recipe's flat transfer maps canvas level 0 to display 1.0, 0.25 to about 0.56, 0.5 to about 0.12, and 0.75 or higher to 0. Bright wave colour on the canvas therefore comes out dark or complementary in the picture. Empty canvas shows as near-white. The settled-state estimate is yellow (canvas blue, display about (1,1,0)), but the warp only lifts blue by about 0.000234 per frame. At 5 s the background should therefore still be near-white with at most a faint cream or yellow tint that is growing slowly, and the strokes should read as dark or colour-inverted rainbow lines. Treat that as the most likely reading. The recipe marks the warp and composite palettes as unresolved, and the audio colour routes below change tint and brightness.

Motion. The feedback map is a uniform zoom whose factor oscillates between about 0.83 and 1.255 per step (a sum of two sinusoids around 1.042; content contracts or expands by roughly -17% to +25% per step), plus a uniform rotation of up to +/-0.05 rad per step, and no translation (dx, dy = 0). The zoom centre wanders: cy stays within 0.4-0.6, while cx swings over a very wide range (about -0.7 to 2.3), so the zoom pivot moves off screen and the image swirls and slides sideways rather than zooming cleanly about the middle. Warp control rates are modest in seconds (zoom changes by at most about 0.067 per second, rotation by at most about 0.023 rad per second). The sampling-displacement estimate puts the 95th-percentile flow at about 2 viewport widths per second (interval 2.0-2.9), which is fast. The calibrated display estimate is lower: mean flow about 0.20 screen widths per second (0.08-0.40), mean absolute frame difference about 0.084 (0.044-0.144). I would describe it as a fast, churning, turbulent swirl with the strokes constantly stretched and re-folded, with a pulsing breathing quality from the zoom oscillation.

Brightness and contrast. The calibrated display estimate gives mean luminance about 0.30 (10th-90th percentile 0.12-0.54), mean saturation about 0.45 (0.17-0.72) and warm/cool about 0.40 (0.06-0.75, slightly cool, very uncertain). The composite inversion alone suggests a bright, near-white background. The mean-luminance estimate suggests a mid-dark picture on average. These two disagree, and the recipe does not settle which dominates, so overall brightness at 5 s is uncertain: mid-grey overall is the safest guess, with white-to-pale or dark-saturated regions possible depending on how much of the canvas the advected strokes cover. Contrast between inverted stroke colour and background should be high where strokes are present. The feedback gain is about 1 for red and green and about 0.99977 for blue, so trails persist almost indefinitely and are not faded out. Content that is bright on the canvas is shown dark, so the picture does not wash out to a single level. The activity estimate is 0.88 (Intense band, possible Normal-Intense), the estimated brightness-jump rate is high (about 57 per 10 s, interval 18-214), and the mood tags are estimated and uncalibrated. Treat these as rough calibrations, not measurements of this preset.

Audio response. Attenuated bass, mid and treble all feed colour routes in the warp shader, and attenuated bass and treble feed the composite shader, all as thresholded or clamped responses with no linear gain given. Expect tint and brightness shifts of the whole image with the music, estimated at only about 0.085 peak RGB effect per bass peak, with an assumed 4 Hz coherent change. In the mesh warp, bass_att shrinks the x scale (sx = 1 - max(0, bass_att - 1.2), down to 0.2) and treb_att shrinks the y scale in the same way. Loud bass or treble therefore squeezes the feedback sampling along one axis, with the effect at its strongest in loud sections, and these terms do nothing at ordinary levels (bass_att near 1 is below the 1.2 threshold). The waves also read audio-derived per-point values (value1 and value2, vb and vvb, bass_att squared, small weights), which shift stroke colour phase and the sideways leg positions. No beat-synchronised whole-screen flash is established, and the source model finds no audio switch site, which is not proof that none exists.

Uncertainty. The recipe has no engine-measured facets, and I did not use any rendered image. Mean hue shares, mean alpha and position bounds for the waves are null. The wave alpha is gated by per-point state (sw), so how much of each stroke is actually drawn is unknown. The warp and composite palettes are unresolved, and the shader colour response of the warp stage is not quantified. The wave positions depend on value1 and value2 and on the aspect scaling, so the leg spacing I give is approximate. The source gives the sign of the inversion but cannot say how much of the screen the strokes cover at 5 s, so the overall light-versus-dark balance is genuinely open. The motion figures are nominal ceilings and assumed rates, not observed speed. This is an approximate recipe for a similar effect, not a pixel-exact description.
