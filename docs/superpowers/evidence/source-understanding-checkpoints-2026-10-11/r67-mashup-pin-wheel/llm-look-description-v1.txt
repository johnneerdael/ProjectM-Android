LLM look description from the SDK recipe, source parts only (before capture, r67 v1; the recipe's engine-measured facets block was deliberately not used):

Core picture: a swirling, collapsing feedback tunnel built from one loop-shaped rainbow line. The main drawn element is a custom wave of 512 points tracing a closed elliptical or Lissajous-like loop (roughly x 0.15-0.86, y 0.05-0.96, so it fills most of the height). Its radius is modulated by the audio waveform samples (value1/value2), so the loop wobbles with the music. Its colour runs through the whole hue wheel along the loop (green, yellow, pink, cyan, red and blue in roughly equal shares, full saturation), and the colours slowly drift over time at a source rate of about 0.1 per second. A 100-sided polygon (effectively a big disc, radius about 0.73, centred) is also drawn in white with texture settings. How much of it shows is unresolved, so I would expect a large soft central disc or glow, with low confidence. The built-in waveform line is authored almost fully transparent (alpha 0.001, black), so it should be practically invisible.

Motion and feedback: every frame the previous image is pulled hard toward the centre (zoom 0.845, about 15% contraction per step) and rotated 0.1 rad per step, with no net drift. The pivot point itself wanders on a slow sinusoidal path (cx and cy swing roughly between -0.3 and 1.3), so the whirlpool centre glides around the screen instead of sitting still. Two warp shader stages add self-advection driven by a blur and noise lookup, which should smear and tug the swirling trails into fluid, flowing, slightly turbulent streaks. Trails persist for a long time (flat-content gain about 0.999, half-life of order 10 s, blue fading faster than red and green). Repeated copies of the loop therefore spiral inward into a dense whorl or tunnel, and the exact density is uncertain. The composite stage applies a two-direction relief (emboss) filter, so edges and texture stand out like lit relief, while flat areas read as a single dim level.

Colour and brightness: the display is passed through a cycling corner-shade tint (red, green and blue channels cycle with periods of about 14.6, 19.6 and 16.2 s, each ranging 0.67-1.0). The hue placement is random per preset load, so I will not name one fixed tint. The source steady state leans a dim yellowish (about 0.12/0.11/0.095) and is a lower bound only. The calibrated display estimate puts mean luminance around 0.25, with a wide 10th-90th percentile interval of 0.07-0.49, so it is moderate rather than dark or blown out. Saturation is estimated at about 0.75 (interval 0.48-1.0), and warm versus cool is balanced (about 0.02, interval -0.31 to +0.38). The rainbow loop gives vivid accents on a dimmer, tinted, embossed field.

Audio reaction: the only established audio route is the continuous waveform shaping the loop (and the nearly invisible built-in line). There are no bass, mid or treble gains, no thresholded beat triggers and no random per-frame redraws in the recipe. So expect a continuously wobbling loop that follows the signal, not beat-synchronised pulses. The persistent swirl runs regardless of the music. Flashing is unknown. The estimate gives about 0.1 global brightness jumps per 10 s (interval 0-3), and the source model does not show a flash mechanism. Overall motion is moderate to fast (estimated flow about 0.17 screen widths per second, interval 0.07-0.36, activity about 0.39 with a wide possible range from Chill to Intense). It reads as a psychedelic, organic, liquid-like swirl. Everything here is an estimate from source maths, and the actual look will differ with drawn content, audio and the random hue placement.
