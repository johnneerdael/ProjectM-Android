LLM look description from the SDK recipe, source parts only (before capture, r103 v1; the recipe's engine-measured facets block was deliberately not used):

Core picture. "suksma - assheadish lice" is, by construction, a full-screen feedback picture rather than a drawing of a few clean objects. Every frame the previous image is resampled through a warp shader, then a thick additive two-trace audio waveform and two polygons are drawn on top, and a composite shader displays the result through a relief/advection pass. The composite has three proven constructions: gradient-driven flow along image gradients (reaction-diffusion or fluid-like), an embossed "local slopes" relief where edges and texture stand out and flat areas read as one level, and self-advection (content steering its own sampling coordinates). The recipe tags it Organic / Liquid. About 5 seconds in I expect a smeared, flowing, embossed-looking field that fills the screen, not a dark background with a few lines. Drawn elements: (1) a built-in wave of mode 7 (two parallel traces following the left and right waveform) centred at y=0.5 with a normal offset of 0.25 between the traces, thick and additive, but with authored alpha of only 0.001 (wave_a), so by itself it is very faint; it is max-normalised, so the colour that does show is full-strength for its largest channel. Because the recipe gives no usable clamp on how the faint alpha reaches the screen, I cannot say how visible these two lines are. (2) shape_0, a 16-sided polygon with a huge radius (100 NDC) and very low centre alpha (0.05); its centre colour is magenta-pink (0.7, 0, 0.4) and its edge colour blue (0, 0.1, 1). With that radius it covers the whole screen, so it acts as a faint pink-to-blue wash rather than a visible shape. Its centre wanders around (0.5, 0.5) within roughly 0.38 to 0.62 on both axes and it spins up to 6 rad/s (+/-6 rad, period about 6.3 s), but a screen-filling wash will show that mostly as a shifting tint. (3) shape_3, a 20-sided polygon at the exact centre (0.5, 0.5), radius 0.967 (almost screen-height), rotated 0.628 rad, stationary, with a red centre (alpha 1) fading to a green edge with alpha 0, and a texture request (zoom 0.73, angle 0.628). Whether its texture is bound is not verified. If it shows, it is a big centred red-to-green disc-like twenty-gon that fades out towards its rim.

Colour. The recipe cannot name one final palette. Source ingredients: shape_0 blue/purple/pink; shape_3 grayscale/red/orange/yellow/green; the warp and composite palettes are unresolved. What the recipe does give is how the shaders map flat canvas levels. The warp shader multiplies flat content by 0 (gain 0 in all channels) and returns an offset of about (0.66, 0.64, 0.63), a light neutral grey, and it wraps levels with a modulo: levels step by about -0.004 per frame and wrap, so the canvas colours cycle through the whole range about every 253 frames, roughly 8.4 s, rather than fading. That is continuous colour cycling. The composite then shows the canvas through a time-varying palette (flat_transfer, averaged over t=2, 5, 8 s): canvas level 0 gives deep blue (0, 0, 0.12); level 0.25 gives near-white (0.98, 0.97, 0.97); level 0.5 gives a warm beige/tan (0.63, 0.60, 0.52) with a large spread between sample times (so it shifts a lot); level 0.75 gives nearly black (0.006, 0.05, 0.004); level 1.0 gives a dark, slightly cyan grey (0.06, 0.09, 0.11). So as the canvas level cycles, I expect banded or posterised-looking regions stepping through near-white, tan, black and dark blue, with the green channel and the tan band moving most over time. The composite also multiplies by the cycling corner shades (hue shader): red, green and blue cycle with periods of about 14.6, 19.6 and 16.2 s, each ranging between 0.67 and 1.0, with four corner tints blended across the screen and the placement random at each load, so a slow corner-to-corner tint drift is likely but the specific hues are not fixed by the source. The steady-state estimate says the settled display is at least white-ish and its hue family is "grayscale"; its level is only a lower bound. Source-calibrated palette numbers: mean saturation about 0.41 (10 to 90 percent interval 0.13 to 0.68), warm/cool about +0.03 (-0.31 to +0.38), i.e. no clear warm or cool bias. The recipe's mood scores lean slightly warm (54 versus 46 cold) but that is an editable assumption. Overall I expect a mixed, moderately saturated image whose dominant tones are pale/white and tan with dark blue and black accents; I do not know the exact hues.

Motion. The warp contracts the image towards the centre every frame by 0.0006 to 0.092 percent per step (zoom 0.9999 with zoomexp 0.109, so the rate grows with radius; the strongest contraction at the edges). Rotation is effectively zero (about 1e-323 rad per step). A constant drift is applied on top: dx = -0.02 (about -288 px/s horizontally, content moving left in the recipe's pixel convention at its reference size) and dy about -0.01 (-0.012 to -0.008, about -115 to -173 px/s, with an extremely slow 628-s wobble). The recipe says the warp map moves content +dx right, +dy down; with negative values the content moves left and up. Those pixel speeds are the sampling-map ceilings at the recipe's reference resolution, combined with zoom, and visible motion depends on content and decay. The warp has an additional position-dependent deformation term (warp strength 11.2 in most places, 11.8 in the upper part and inner region, with a 0.6 rad/s sine on the inner top half) which I read as a wavy distortion, but its visible form is unknown. Added to that, the composite self-advects and flows along gradients, so patterns should smear and creep rather than translate rigidly. shape_0 spins fast (up to 6 rad/s) and its centre orbits (period set by time) with a bass-threshold jump; shape_3 is stationary. The colour cycling (about 8.4 s) is another slow motion. The source-calibrated display estimate predicts mean flow of about 0.31 screen widths per second (0.15 to 0.60) and a mean frame-to-frame difference of 0.084 (0.044 to 0.144), i.e. a lot of motion. Flow speed and frame difference come from a calibrated model, not from rendering this preset.

Brightness and contrast. The calibrated estimate gives mean luminance 0.37 (10 to 90 percent range 0.18 to 0.60), so middle-brightness, but with a wide range from fairly dark to quite bright. The recipe records no usable feedback decay (decay null, trail half-life null), because the custom warp discards the decay control, and the warp flat transfer shows a gain of 0 on flat content, so old content is not simply kept as fading trails. Contrast is probably high: the relief pass turns edges into bright/dark rims and flat areas into one level, and the composite palette jumps between near-white, tan and near-black. The estimate lists about 11.8 global brightness jumps per 10 s (3 to 46), which means frequent brightness changes. This is a hint that the picture is lively; the recipe does not certify any flash, and its flash and spike features are marked as assumed. A black waveform line over feedback would cut what it covers, but the wave here is faint.

Audio response. Bass, mid and treble all feed the built-in wave's x position (continuous response, no stated gain), so the two traces should sway sideways with the music, but at alpha 0.001 this may be hard to see. Bass moves shape_0's centre in x and y, thresholded or clamped (it only reacts when bass is above a threshold), so on a bass hit the large pink-blue wash should jump position and tint. Packed volume and attenuated volume feed the warp shader's red, green and blue values, all thresholded or clamped. This means louder music should shift the canvas colours (and so the cycling palette position), possibly the biggest audible link in this preset, but its strength is not given. The two traces follow the actual left and right waveform shape. The calibrated activity percentile is 0.87 (0.49 to 0.99), band Intense, with Normal also possible. The recipe's approximate mood is "Normal" with Intense possible, medium confidence, with intensity score about 59, psychedelic about 50, and smoothness 0. I would call it energetic, organic, flowing and somewhat psychedelic, with those labels being approximate interpretations, not strict eligibility (the strict source model records no eligible mood group).

Uncertainty. Several things are unknown: the actual final palette and which hues dominate; how visible the nearly transparent waveform is; whether shape_3's texture binds and what it looks like; the true shape and strength of the warp deformation; the real trail persistence (null); the real speed of visible motion (the numbers above are sampling-map ceilings and calibrated estimates, not observed); how strong any audio response is; whether the frequent brightness jumps amount to visible flashing (not certified either way); the random per-load placement of the hue shader; and the mean encoded luma, which the mood layer lists as unknown. The recipe states it is an approximate recipe, not a pixel-exact specification, and the display estimate is a calibrated prediction, not a rendered measurement. Exotic values such as rotation 1e-323 and the huge shape_0 radius of 100 are literal source values, and I treat the former as zero rotation and the latter as a full-screen wash.
