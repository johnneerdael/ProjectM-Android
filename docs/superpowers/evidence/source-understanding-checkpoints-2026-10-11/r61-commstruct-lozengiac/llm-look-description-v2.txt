LLM look description from the SDK recipe, source parts only (r61 v2; the recipe's engine-measured facets block was deliberately not used):

Core picture: a small, compact figure sits in the middle of the screen, not a full-screen pattern. Two drawn elements make it. One is a built-in circular waveform ring (an audio-deformed circle, centred at 0.5,0.5, slowly rotating at about 0.2 rad/s). Its outline wobbles with the right-channel waveform, with a radial gain of 0.4. Its authored alpha is very low (about 0.001), so the ring itself is only a faint wisp. The other is a custom 512-point wave whose points lie in a tight closed-form patch, x and y both about 0.4 to 0.6, a small cluster or lozenge-like shape around the centre. Nothing is drawn near the edges. Whatever fills the rest of the screen has to come from feedback and blur, and the recipe cannot say how far that spreads. Full-screen coverage is unresolved.

Colour: the custom wave points are strongly coloured. About 47% read as blue, 46% as yellow, 6% as purple and 1% as green, with mean saturation about 0.73 and full alpha. Blue and yellow are the two main hues. The composite then multiplies the whole image by cycling corner shades. Red, green and blue each cycle with periods of roughly 14.6 s, 19.6 s and 16.2 s, over a 0.67 to 1.0 range. Which hue appears in which corner is random per preset load, so I can't name one fixed tint. The composite's flat-canvas palette is also time-varying, with a slow per-channel shift, and the settled background leans dark purple. The steady-state hue family is purple, at a very low level of about 0.04, 0.03, 0.05 RGB. That level is only a lower bound. Overall the picture should be a dark purplish ground with blue and yellow points and a tint that drifts slowly. The exact palette is conditional on that random placement.

Feedback and motion: trails are very short. The warp transfer keeps only about 45% of the previous frame per frame, so the half-life is about one frame (about 0.03 s). Content fades almost immediately and nothing builds up into long streaks, except that very dim content (level around 0.25) is kept whole. There is no drift (dx and dy are both 0), no rotation and no mirroring or tiling. The only warp motion is a radial zoom that varies with distance from the centre, from about 0.77 to 1.11 per step. It zooms about the screen centre, in or out depending on radius. The warp also applies an 8-tap neighbourhood blur whose radius grows toward the edges and changes with audio. That softens the picture into haze and can smear it into star or X shapes. Those two effects pull the figure into a softly smeared, radially stretched form, and a blur radius that tracks audio can look erratic. The recipe's engine-calibrated estimate puts flow at about 0.24 screen widths per second, with a wide interval (0.10 to 0.47). Treat that as an estimate, not a measurement.

Audio: the ring outline follows the waveform continuously. Bass drives red, green and blue in the warp shader, and smoothed bass and treble drive red, green and blue in the composite. These are thresholded or clamped brightness and colour routes, with no linear gain known, so how strongly they show is unresolved. I'd expect brightness and tint to pump with the music. The recipe also records a brightness-cycle rate of about 14 Hz in the warp shader, which is a source mechanism, not a proven visible flash. The calibrated estimate gives frequent global brightness jumps, about 55 per 10 s, with a very wide interval (18 to 208), and a high activity percentile (about 0.93, Intense, possibly Normal). The tags are smooth, hypnotic, reactive and flashing. Possible flashing is flagged, so it should not be treated as safe for flash-sensitive viewers.

Brightness and randomness: the estimated mean luminance is about 0.29 (interval 0.10 to 0.52), moderate to dark, with a mostly dark ground and brighter coloured points. Saturation is estimated at about 0.75, though its interval is wide. The warm/cool balance is near neutral (0.05, interval -0.29 to 0.40). The random part is the per-load hue placement of the composite shading. The recipe lists no per-frame random redraws. The chaotic elements are the audio-driven blur radius and the threshold-style colour responses. The recipe is approximate, not a pixel specification, and the strict model assigns no firm mood band.
