LLM look description from the SDK recipe, source parts only (before capture, r62 v1; the recipe's engine-measured facets block was deliberately not used):

Core picture. The preset is built from three drawn elements over a feedback canvas: a dot-mode stereo waveform (mode 3, "stereo_xy_trace_volume_alpha", centred at 0.5/0.5, dots rather than a line, not thick, not additive), one white four-sided shape at the screen centre, and a custom warp and composite shader whose colour logic the recipe could not resolve. The waveform is an X/Y trace of the left and right channels, so it should read as a scatter of dots forming a wandering, Lissajous-like cloud around the middle that changes shape with the stereo content. The shape is a square with a very large nominal radius (about 2.67 in screen units), so it is far bigger than the screen. It is drawn at low alpha (0.2) with a white centre fading to white at alpha 0 at the edge, plus a faint black border (alpha 0.1). Most likely it acts as a soft, slightly textured veil or flash over the whole picture rather than a visible square. Its rotation advances with a per-frame "spin" variable (+0.01 per step), so any pattern it carries turns slowly. How strongly it shows is unresolved. The recipe has no mirror, tiling, tunnel or kaleidoscope structure (symmetry score 0).

Feedback and motion. The warp is a pure radial zoom about the screen centre. Zoom is 1 + 0.01*bass + 0.1*treb*0.2*(4*rad)-style term. It never drops below 1 and tops out near 1.09, and it varies with distance from the centre, so the previous image expands outward, more at the edges than near the centre. There is no rotation, no translation and no warp distortion in the warp map. The result should be a gentle outward flow from the centre, with the dots smearing into short radial streaks. How long trails last is not resolved: the decay is null because a custom warp shader is in use. The flat-colour transfer test says white is kept whole, black stays black and mid-greys are changed strongly. The 0.5 level is almost erased (retention about 0.001) and 0.25 and 0.75 are kept at about 0.59. That points to a posterised or banded response in the shader: very dark and very bright content persists, mid-tones are cut. I am unsure what this looks like in practice and it may produce a stepped, contrasty look. Motion is smooth overall (smoothness estimate 90 out of 100) with modest speed. The source speed estimate is only an assumed 0.3, with an interval of 0 to 1.

Colour. The waveform dot colour is the one clear colour source. Each channel is 0.5 + 0.2*sin(time * rate) plus a band term. Red uses time*1.33 with bass*0.3, green uses time*0.3 (a slow sine) with treble*0.3, and blue uses time*0.988 with mid*0.3. With max-normalisation on, the brightest channel is scaled to 1, so the dots should be fully saturated-looking or bright pastel colours that drift slowly through the hue wheel over several seconds. Bass pushes toward red, treble toward green and mid toward blue. The dots' alpha is replaced by a treble-squared-based value, not the authored 0.8. What the shaders do to that colour is unresolved. The recipe's own steady-state and palette numbers are grayscale: palette saturation 0 and hue variety 0, from a flat-field model that the recipe says does not describe the real picture. The composite flat-transfer shows mid-levels mapped to green-ish tints (0.25 maps to about 0.60/0.82/0.46) and 0.5 and above to white. So I expect bright, whitish content with a possible pale green cast in dim regions, and coloured dots over it. I treat the colour as uncertain and not clearly multicolour.

Audio reaction. Bass adds up to 0.01 per bass unit to the zoom, which is small. Treble adds a larger radius-dependent zoom term, with no gain given in the recipe. Treble, mid and bass each shift one colour channel of the dots by 0.3 times the band level, and treble also drives the dot alpha. This is continuous and not thresholded, so I expect the dots to breathe in brightness and hue with the music rather than flash on beats. The dots' positions follow the actual stereo waveform. There is no found beat switch, and random-per-load and random redraw lists are empty (null), so I see nothing random per load. The state evolution of the shape spin is the only hidden state.

Brightness and chaos. The recipe's non-measured estimates are weak. The mood block has low confidence with mostly assumed values: intensity 16.5 (interval 5 to 100), psychedelic 14. Strict mood bands are empty. The display_estimate block, which I was told not to use, is ignored here. The brightness level is therefore unknown. One caution is that a white shape of huge radius at alpha 0.2 could wash the screen toward bright grey/white each frame, and white is preserved by the warp, so the canvas may accumulate toward white rather than black. I cannot confirm that from the source alone. Flashing is assumed low (coherent transitions assumed 0.8 to 4 Hz with small deltas 0.03 to 0.3). Nothing indicates chaos, though the posterising warp response is the least understood piece and could give surprising results.
