LLM look description from the SDK recipe, source parts only (before capture, r110 v1; the recipe's engine-measured facets block was deliberately not used):

Core picture. A feedback preset built from a continuously re-fed canvas with grey drawing layers over it. The main drawn elements are two four-sided shapes (shape_1 and shape_2) centred at (0.5, 0.5) and stationary, each with a nominal NDC radius of about 2.0, so each is much larger than the screen and acts as a full-screen wash, not a visible object. One has fill alpha 0.2 and the other 0.4, both with a faint white border (alpha 0.1). Two custom point layers (wave_0 and wave_1, 512 points each) draw grey points at very low alpha (mean about 0.016), and a built-in two-trace waveform (mode 7, two parallel traces offset about 0.25 either side of centre) is drawn in grey 0.65 additive at alpha about 0.001. The traces are close to invisible by themselves. A viewer would see mostly a soft, hazy, textured-looking field, with faint grey point dust and faint horizontal waveform traces accumulating in the feedback. I would not expect crisp objects. The name suggests glass-like worms, but I did not use the name as evidence. The recipe gives no particle tracking (independent particle state is not proven), and the point positions depend on per-frame state that the recipe does not resolve.

Colour. The recipe's palette is mostly grey. The point layers are 100% grayscale and the wave is grey. The steady-state hue family is grayscale, with a settled display colour of white as a lower bound. The shapes' fill and feedback palette are marked unresolved, and their colour channels are driven by bass, mid and treble. The shape colours may therefore tint the image, but the recipe gives no reached hues. The display estimate puts mean saturation at about 0.09 (interval 0 to 0.36) and warm/cool near neutral (0.04, interval about -0.30 to +0.39). I would describe it as a mostly neutral white-to-grey image, possibly with weak, unpredictable tints that shift with the music. I cannot name a dominant hue.

Motion. Each frame the previous image is resampled with a small radial zoom: the whole image expands from the screen centre by about 0.014% to 0.18% per step, faster toward the edges because zoomexp is about 2.99. It is also rotated by 4.1888 rad per step. That is 240 degrees, or equivalently minus 120 degrees. This is my own arithmetic, not a recipe statement. If it is right, each frame turns the previous image by one third of a turn, so repeated feedback would show a three-fold rotational pattern that flickers in orientation, not a slow smooth spin. The recipe itself only says the rate is per step and that visible motion depends on content, decay and composition. Drift (dx, dy) is 0, and the shapes do not move. The sampling displacement ceiling is about 2.0 viewport units per step, which is a ceiling and not a typical speed. The display estimate gives mean flow about 0.16 screen widths per second (interval 0.05 to 0.33) and mean frame-to-frame difference about 0.047 (0.015 to 0.095), which suggests moderate, busy movement.

Brightness and contrast. Decay is 1.0, so nothing fades from the feedback, and content can build up. The composite brightens strongly: gamma redraw gain is up to 2.96x before clamping. It also overlays a copy of the image magnified 1.03x at 50% opacity, with no flip. A border darkens the screen centre slightly (up to 3/32 per frame). The display estimate gives mean luminance about 0.42 (interval 0.23 to 0.66). Overall I expect a mid-to-bright, fairly low-contrast, hazy image that may drift toward washed-out or saturated white. Global brightness jumps are estimated at about 1.7 per 10 seconds (interval 0 to 9). The activity estimate is Normal (0.49), but the interval is so wide that Chill and Intense are also possible. The strict source model has no eligible mood group.

Audio response. The only clear routes are bass, mid and treble into the shapes' fill and perimeter colour channels (R, G, B). All are listed as threshold or clamped responses with no linear gain given, so I can say colour and brightness of the big shapes react to the music, but not how strongly or whether on the beat. The built-in wave follows the left and right waveform, but its alpha is about 0.001, so its audio shape is barely visible. The recipe lists no switch sites and no random redraws. The flash-related numbers are assumptions, not measured. I cannot claim a visible beat pulse.

Uncertainty. Several things are unresolved. First, whether the huge shapes are textured, and with what image. Their texture zoom values are given but the bound texture is not verified. Second, the actual colours of the shape layers and of the feedback. Third, the effect of the 4.19 rad rotation under real sampling and interpolation, which could look like fast shimmer, noise or a rotationally symmetric pattern. Fourth, how much the faint point and wave layers show up against the accumulated wash. Fifth, how fast the image saturates, because decay 1.0 plus gamma 2.96 could push much of the screen toward white. The display estimate carries wide intervals. It is a calibrated statistical guess from other presets, not a measurement of this one.
