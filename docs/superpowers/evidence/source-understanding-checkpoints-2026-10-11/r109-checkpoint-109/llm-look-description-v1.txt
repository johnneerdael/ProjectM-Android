LLM look description from the SDK recipe, source parts only (before capture, r109 v1; the recipe's engine-measured facets block was deliberately not used):

Core picture. About five seconds in, I expect a full-screen, feedback-built texture rather than a clean drawing on a dark background. The only drawn element is one thin, additive built-in waveform line (mode 6, "angled waveform trace", not dots, not thick) anchored at the screen centre (0.5, 0.5) that follows the left audio channel. Everything else comes from feedback. The warp stage adds a finer copy of the previous image and subtracts a coarser blur every frame (pattern growth), and also advects the image along its own gradients and colours. That should grow the thin line into blobs, cells or splatter and smear them into organic, fluid-looking, reaction-diffusion-like structure that spreads over most of the canvas. The composite stage then folds the image with a Cartesian mirror, repeats it in mirrored tiles, and lets the blurred image steer its own sampling. The result should read as a mirrored or tiled, kaleidoscope-like, flowing field. The recipe puts mirror symmetry at about 0.49 and calls that an upper estimate, so the symmetry is imperfect. There is also a thin outer border of about 2% of the screen drawn every frame, but its colour is not resolved (the recipe lists [None, None, None]).

Colour. The line itself is the one fully known colour. Its channels are R 1.0, G 0.75, B 0 at alpha 0.8, and max-normalisation scales that up to full orange-yellow (about 1, 0.75, 0). That is a saturated warm amber seed. The fed-back canvas is not amber, though. The warp keeps red nearly whole (gain 0.9957, halving in roughly 160 frames), holds green at gain 0.99 plus 0.01, so flat green does not fade, and resets blue to about 0.48 every frame (blue gain about 0). Flat areas therefore settle in about 2.8 s to a green-teal canvas level (about 0.0, 1.0, 0.48; steady state about 0.004, 0.98, 0.47). That canvas is not shown directly. The composite maps it through a time-varying palette (flat_transfer) in which pure black and full white canvas both display as black and mid levels display as dim, fairly neutral colour. The composite also multiplies by four corner shades that cycle with periods of about 14.6 s (R), 19.6 s (G) and 16.2 s (B), each ranging 0.67 to 1. Their placement is random per load, so I cannot say which hue appears where. The steady-state estimate gives a blue-leaning, dim base (display about 0.22, 0.25, 0.28, flagged as a lower bound with a large spread of about 0.46). My best guess is blue/teal/grey-cyan textured areas with slowly drifting, differently tinted regions, plus amber-yellow wave strokes as the warm accent. The calibrated estimate gives mean saturation about 0.46 (interval 0.18 to 0.73) and warm/cool about 0.11 (interval -0.22 to 0.47), which is roughly neutral and consistent with a mixed palette. I cannot name a dominant hue with confidence.

Motion. The warp has no rotation (0) and no translation (dx = dy = 0), and the centre is fixed at (0.5, 0.5). The only global transform is a uniform zoom about the screen centre, driven by audio. Its nominal envelope is 0.41 to 1.59 per step (up to 58.6% contraction or expansion), but that is a bound on the range and not a typical value. Typical zoom is unknown, and I would expect a breathing in-out pulse at the centre rather than a constant tunnel. On top of that, the shader advection (offsets up to about +/-0.015 in x and +/-0.021 in y of UV) and the gradient-driven flow should make structures slither and smear, and the mirrored tiling will make them look as if they flow symmetrically. The calibrated estimate gives mean flow about 0.23 screen widths per second (interval 0.10 to 0.46), mean frame-to-frame difference about 0.083 (interval 0.043 to 0.143), and an activity percentile of 0.76 (interval 0.30 to 0.96, band Intense, with Chill/Normal also possible). I would therefore describe it as fairly busy and flowing, moderate to fast, not static, but I cannot give a certain speed.

Brightness and contrast. The calibrated mean luminance is about 0.49 (interval 0.30 to 0.73), so mid-brightness and not near-black. Because the pattern-growth stage fills the canvas with fine texture, I expect contrast to be fairly high locally, with bright amber strokes and edges on the mid-dim tinted field. The composite maps both black and full-white canvas levels to black, so some bright regions may drop out into dark gaps. The flat-field level is only a lower bound and does not describe the textured picture. The estimated rate of global brightness jumps is about 3.3 per 10 s with a very wide interval (0.4 to 14.8). The source gives no explicit blackout gate or strobe site, so I would not expect hard flashing, but I cannot rule out noticeable pulsing from the zoom and the colour-cycling corner shades.

Audio response. The recipe finds six routes, and all of them go into the uniform feedback zoom: bass, mid, treble and their attenuated versions. The expression appears to weight bass most heavily (about 1.2 on bass and 0.4 on bass_att, against about 0.1 each on treble, mid and their attenuated versions), but the recipe leaves linear_gain null and I did not execute the formula, so the strength is unresolved. The responses are continuous, with no thresholds or clamps found, so I expect smooth in-out zoom pulsing that follows the music, probably most strongly on bass. The waveform line's shape follows the left channel, so its wiggle tracks the audio. I found no audio route to brightness or colour (the mood block records bass peak colour effect 0.0), and no beat flashes by design. Frame-to-frame brightness changes would come indirectly through zoom and feedback.

Uncertainty. The recipe is an approximate source description with no rendered images. Unknown or only conditional items: the final palette and dominant hue (the warp and composite colours are null and unresolved, and the hue placement is random per load); the typical zoom amplitude and speed (only a +/-58.6% enclosure and an unknown curve); the border colour; how much of the screen the pattern-growth texture actually covers at five seconds, and whether the line seeds enough content to fill it; how strongly the tiling and mirroring read in the picture (symmetry score 0.49 is an upper estimate); and whether the dim display-level lower bound or the calibrated 0.49 luminance is closer to the truth. The calibrated estimates carry wide intervals (brightness jumps especially) and are not a rendering of this preset. The likelihood of visible strobing is unknown, with an assumed range of 0 to 4 Hz flagged as unknown evidence. Five seconds after load the feedback may still be building up (the flat canvas takes about 2.8 s to settle), so the picture may be sparser or less saturated than the steady state I describe.
