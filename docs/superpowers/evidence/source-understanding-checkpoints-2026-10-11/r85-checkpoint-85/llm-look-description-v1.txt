LLM look description from the SDK recipe, source parts only (before capture, r85 v1; the recipe's engine-measured facets block was deliberately not used):

Core picture. This looks like a busy, swirling feedback tunnel with a few bright drawn elements riding on top of it. The drawn layers are: a dotted stereo "XY trace" of the audio (built-in wave mode 2, dots, centred at 0.5/0.5, so scattered points that follow the left/right waveform); a 512-point custom wave whose positions are a closed-form function of sample index, time and bass (a curve or ring-like radial figure, but not proven to be an exact circle); and two large 100-sided polygons (effectively circles, NDC radius about 0.36 and 0.33) whose centres wander on independent sine paths across roughly 0.14-0.86 horizontally and 0.15-0.85 vertically (periods about 2.6 s and 4.1 s). A third small 4-sided shape sits at y=1.8, which is off-screen, so it should not be visible (it is stationary). The two big circles have no visible border (border alpha 0) and an inner alpha of 1 fading toward an outer alpha of 0 with white inner/outer colours, so expect soft glowing discs rather than hard-edged ones. Their exact on-screen fill colour is not resolved in the recipe (palette "unresolved"; one has a texture zoom that suggests a texture request, which is not a verified bound image). I cannot say how much of the screen the discs cover.

Feedback and motion. The warp step is strong and spatially varying: zoom depends on radius and angle, with a rotation term that also depends on radius, so the picture twists and breathes with distance from the centre rather than moving uniformly. Zoom and rotation are partly driven by smoothed bass, mid and treble, and by some thresholded switches. The recipe's own numbers for them are unresolved, so I cannot give a speed. An 8-tap neighbourhood blur (radius varying with screen position and with audio) smears content into a soft haze. The composite stage adds self-advection (the image steers its own sampling), noise-texture displacement (turbulent shimmer) and a periodic wobble. Together that suggests fluid, smeared, swirling structure. Trails are short. The flat-content warp transfer gives a gain of about 0.45 per frame, so uniform bright content halves each frame, and mid-level content is kept better than bright content (retention is 1.0 at level 0.25, about 0.82 at 0.5, about 0.45 above 0.75). Old marks should therefore die quickly. The impression of long streaks must come from the warp and advection moving content, not from a slow fade. The recipe lists no mirror or kaleidoscope symmetry (symmetry score 0).

Colour. The built-in dotted wave cycles its own colour smoothly: red, green and blue each sweep about 0.15-1.15 with periods of about 5.6 s, 5.1 s and 4.7 s. Over time it passes through green, blue, pink, orange, cyan and yellow (mostly green and blue by share), so a slow drifting hue is expected. These are raw source curves, not confirmed displayed colours. The custom wave's points are a mix of greys and hues (grey about 19%, red 18%, blue 16%, green 14%, yellow 13%, with smaller shares of pink, orange, purple and cyan), moderately saturated (mean saturation about 0.44, full alpha). That is multicolour confetti-like dots, not a single hue. The warp shader adds a bass-dependent term on all three colour channels, so bass tints or brightens the field, but its strength is not resolved. A thin red border band covering about 1% of the screen is blended in every frame at alpha 0.16. The composite maps flat canvas levels through a palette that goes from black through a dim cool blue-green (at level 0.25), to a warm tan/orange (0.5), to pinkish (0.75), to white (1.0). So low-level haze tends toward dark teal/cyan, while brighter regions shift warm, then pink, then white. The source steady-state hue family for the settled background is cyan, but it is only a low-level lower bound (local RGB about 0.02-0.04), so the dark background should be a faint teal-cyan, not pure black. Overall palette dominance is conditional and unverified.

Audio reaction. Bass, mid and treble (the smoothed versions) drive zoom, and bass and mid drive rotation. All of these go through thresholds or clamps, so the tunnel's pulse and twist probably kick in and out as levels cross thresholds rather than responding linearly. Raw bass changes the colour channels in the warp shader and feeds the custom wave's positions. The XY trace follows the left and right waveform continuously. No linear gains are exported, so I cannot say how strong any reaction is or whether it lands on the beat.

Brightness and energy. The source-only potential mood is "Intense" but uncalibrated, and the strict model gives no eligible mood band. The shapes' centres move at up to about 1.04 viewport units per second, which is a ceiling, not a typical speed. Because the feedback decay is strong, the output stays fairly dim overall. The bright layers are the white-ish dotted waves and discs. I would expect a dark teal field with bright, shifting-colour dots and rings, but the overall brightness is unresolved from source facts alone. There is a possible flashing or brightness-pulse mechanism through the bass colour terms and the thresholded zoom/rotation switches; its frequency and visibility are unknown, so I do not claim strobing.

Random, chaotic or undefined. No per-load random branches, random redraws, random texture slots or undefined (NaN) shader outputs are listed. The noise texture in the composite is a named request, and I do not know which noise image or how strongly it displaces. Several warp controls (zoom, rotation, translation, scale) are marked unresolved or unknown, and they depend on per-pixel state variables. Fine detail, true coverage and exact motion speed are therefore uncertain, and the real image may look more chaotic or smeared than described.
