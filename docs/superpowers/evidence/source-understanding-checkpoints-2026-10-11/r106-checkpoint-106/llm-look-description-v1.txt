LLM look description from the SDK recipe, source parts only (before capture, r106 v1; the recipe's engine-measured facets block was deliberately not used):

Core picture. This is a feedback-driven preset with a single drawn element: one thick, non-additive, closed ring (built-in wave mode 0, "audio_deformed_circle") centred on the screen (wave_x 0.5, wave_y 0.5, /render/layers/1/builtin_wave). Its effective base radius is about 0.2 model units (effective_base_radius_model_units 0.19999998807907104), so roughly a fifth of the screen, with the outline wobbling radially with the right-channel waveform (sample_radial_gain 0.4) and the whole shape turning slowly at a nominal 0.2 rad/s (about 3 s per radian, roughly 31 s per turn). No custom shapes, motion-vector grid, mirrors or random textures are present. Around that ring, the feedback stack does most of the visual work. The mesh warp expands the previous frame outward from the centre every step (zoom 1.03, zoomexp 13.67, giving a sampling zoom from about 1.002 near the centre to about 2.39 at the far edge, i.e. 0.2% to 139% per step). The ring is therefore smeared outward into concentric, flowing bands that stretch ever faster toward the edges, while a custom warp shader adds image-driven self-advection, and the custom composite adds an embossed-relief look (4-direction slope shading), non-mirrored repeat tiling, and further self-advection and gradient flow. Expect a fluid, smeared, organic, tunnel-ish outward flow with embossed edges and some repeated or tiled structure, rather than a crisp geometric ring. The recipe does not say how strong the tiling is, how many repeats appear, or how recognisable the ring is after the composite.

Colour. The ring itself changes hue over time: its red, green and blue sine channels (ranges -0.5 to 0.5, periods about 11.1 s, 10.2 s and 9.45 s, phase offsets 0.565, 0.615, 0.665) are clamped and then max-normalised, so the brightest channel is always at full strength and the hue drifts. Over 120 s the closed-form hue spend is roughly blue 25%, green 23%, red 17%, grey/white 11%, pink 7%, orange 7%, cyan 4%, yellow 4%, purple 3%. Mean saturation of the wave colour is about 0.77, with about 8.6 effective hue bins, so at five seconds the ring is a fairly saturated colour (most likely in the blue, green or red families, but any is possible) that is visibly sliding to another hue over a few seconds. Whenever all three channels sit at or below 0.01 the line would be near black; the recipe says this can cause dark phases. However, the picture is not just the ring colour: the composite maps the canvas through a palette. The flat-canvas transfer gives black for black canvas, a warm orange-brown (about 0.39, 0.29, 0.23) for a quarter-level canvas, a darker brown (about 0.20, 0.14, 0.10) for half-level, and black again for 0.75 and full-level canvas. The steady-state estimate is a very dim warm orange glow (display about 0.039, 0.030, 0.025; hue_family orange). So the overall tone is plausibly warm orange-brown shading with the cycling ring colour on top or within it. The recipe cannot say which wins; the shader palette and sampled feedback colours are unresolved, and the flat-areas-only warning applies, so textured areas may look brighter and differently coloured than those flat values.

Motion. The warp has no translation (dx = dy = 0) and no rotation (rot 0), so the feedback only expands from the centre; there is no sideways drift. The recipe's calibrated estimate puts mean flow at about 0.08 screen widths per second (10th to 90th percentile 0.008 to 0.20) and mean frame-to-frame difference at 0.019 (0 to 0.058), which is gentle to moderate. The ring turns slowly (0.2 rad/s), while its hue drifts at up to about 0.31 cycles per second at the 95th percentile. Feedback trails are long: warp gain is about 0.9989 per frame (half-life about 620 frames, 20 s at 30 fps, per-channel nearly identical, not channel selective, linear), so older layers persist for many seconds while being pushed outward and smeared. Content near the edges moves faster than content near the centre. Visible speed is a model-based upper bound only; the mood layer's speed values are assumptions.

Brightness and contrast. Calibrated display estimate: mean luminance about 0.20 (wide interval 0.008 to 0.43), mean saturation about 0.35 (0.07 to 0.62), warm/cool about +0.10 (slightly warm, interval -0.23 to +0.46), global brightness jumps about 0.02 per 10 s (0 to 2.8). So most likely a moderately dark to mid-dark image with a small bright area, soft tonal changes and no strong whole-screen flashing. The shader stages and the non-monotonic flat transfer (bright canvas shown as black) mean contrast could be unusual: mid-level content shows as warm brown while the brightest content might appear dark, giving an embossed, inverted-looking relief. Brightness could be anywhere from near-black to fairly lit; I lean dim-to-medium. Flash and brightness-jump features are unknown or assumed small.

Audio response. The only established audio route is the waveform: the ring outline is deformed continuously by the right-channel waveform (smoothed false, no bands). There are no bass/mid/treble bindings (audio_bindings is empty) and no modelled audio brightness effect (audio.bass_peak_rgb_effect 0). So with music the ring should visibly wiggle with the signal, and louder passages should produce larger ripples in the ring, which then feed the outward smear; the global zoom, colour cycling and warp do not change with the beat in the recipe. No beat-locked pulsing is predicted.

Uncertainty. The recipe is an approximate source description, not a pixel specification. Unresolved: the colour output of the warp and composite shaders, how much of the screen is covered by drawn or tiled structure, the exact brightness (interval from near black to about 0.43 mean luminance), the real visibility of the ring after long feedback and composite, the effect of tiling and relief on legibility, and flashing (transitions unknown). The mood layer is low confidence and internally mixed: the calibrated activity says Chill (activity 0.12, possibly Normal), while the heuristic scores say intensity 48 with a wide interval, psychedelic about 76, smoothness 20, and tags Psychedelic and Organic/Liquid. The strict source model records no eligible mood group, and the gentle-home-TV and focus profiles flag speed, acceleration, jerk and hue rate as violated. I would call it a slow-to-moderate, hue-cycling, smeared and embossed psychedelic flow, with the exact appearance, brightness and tile structure uncertain.
