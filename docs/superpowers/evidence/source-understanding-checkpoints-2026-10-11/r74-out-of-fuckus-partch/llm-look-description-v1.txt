LLM look description from the SDK recipe, source parts only (before capture, r74 v1; the recipe's engine-measured facets block was deliberately not used):

Core picture: a dark, soft, smoky feedback canvas with four custom line-waveform layers (512 points each) drawn into it. The strongest visible drawn element is wave_1, a translucent (mean alpha about 0.5), highly saturated line (mean saturation 0.99) coloured about 86% pink, 11% purple and 3% blue. wave_3 is a very faint grey line (alpha about 0.025) that adds only a subtle haze. wave_0 and wave_2 have dynamic or black authored colours whose reached colours the recipe does not resolve, so I do not claim what they look like. The recipe gives closed-form positions but no position bounds, so I cannot say what shapes the waves trace or how much screen they cover. The image is mostly feedback-smeared pink/magenta strands and glow on a mostly dark background, not a crisp geometric figure. No mirroring or kaleidoscope symmetry is indicated (symmetry score 0).

Colour: the pink/purple wave is the clearest hue cue. The settled feedback colour leans green (steady-state hue family green, very low level, about 0.01 to 0.02 RGB), so a faint greenish cast on the dark background is plausible. The feedback warp strongly favours red and green over blue: blue is essentially erased each frame (gain 0.0004), red keeps about 0.74 and green about 0.90 per frame. Trails therefore skew towards red/yellow-green, and the pink line's blue part dies quickly. The composite shader remaps the canvas through a time-varying palette (it shifts over time, and the recipe lists it as time_varying). Mid-grey levels come out near white or cyan-white and low levels come out pinkish or green, so overall colour will drift and may look more varied than the drawn waves alone. That composite palette is only partly resolved, so treat the exact tints as uncertain. The source-calibrated estimate says moderate saturation (about 0.47, wide interval 0.2 to 0.74) and cool-leaning (warm/cool about -0.57), which sits uneasily with the red-favouring trails. I would not commit to one dominant tint.

Motion and feedback: the warp is a very slow uniform zoom toward the centre (0.049% per frame), with no rotation and no fixed translation, so there is no steady spin or drift. Persistence is short and channel-selective: red halves in about 2 frames, green in about 3 and blue almost immediately, with no long trail. The warp also includes an 8-tap neighbourhood blur, which smears content into soft haze rather than sharp streaks. The composite uses image-driven advection (the picture steers its own sampling coordinates through the blur buffer), giving flowing, smeared, fluid-like distortion. The warp centre wanders on a small time- and mid-driven path (cx and cy have a mid-audio and musictime term). That sets a gentle swirling and wobbling of the zoom origin. The source-calibrated flow estimate is about 0.15 screen widths per second (interval 0.05 to 0.32). I read that as moderate, smooth, fluid motion rather than fast.

Audio: the mid band is the only input. It drives the horizontal and vertical scale of the per-pixel warp field, which is continuous, and its gain is not established. It also drives a thresholded or clamped change in the green channel of the warp shader. In practice, mid-heavy passages should make the smeared structure breathe, stretch or wobble and make green flicker in and out. Bass and treble have no recipe routes. The visible strength is unresolved.

Brightness and flashing: the picture is mostly dark. The calibrated mean luminance is about 0.22 (interval 0.03 to 0.45, so uncertain). The estimated frame-to-frame change is about 0.06, and the estimated global brightness jumps are about 13 per 10 seconds (interval about 4 to 53). Flashing is therefore possible but unresolved, and I would not call it a strobe. The estimated activity is middling (about 0.66, interval 0.14 to 0.96, so Chill to Intense are all possible), with Normal as the nominal band. The recipe's mood assumptions are editable, not calibrated.

Random and chaotic: the recipe lists no random-per-load switches and no random redraws, so I expect the same look on each load, apart from audio. The chaotic parts are the blur-fed advection loop and the unresolved wave_0 and wave_2 colours, which the recipe does not resolve. Treat this whole description as an approximate source-based forecast, not a rendered measurement.
