LLM look description from the SDK recipe, source parts only (before capture, r104 v1; the recipe's engine-measured facets block was deliberately not used):

Core picture. About five seconds in, I expect a full-screen, smeared, fluid-looking field of feedback texture with a thin audio line pair laid over its middle. The base is a feedback canvas that is resampled every frame: it contracts very slightly toward the centre (about 0.02% per step, render.feedback.warp_patterns, radial_zoom_profile), rotates about the centre by 0.02 rad per step (about 1.15 degrees), and slides upward at roughly 115 px/s (dy = -0.008, content moves up). Two shader stages (warp and composite) let the image steer its own sampling coordinates, with composite offsets up to about 0.4 UV, and the composite adds gradient-following flow. The mechanism list names "reaction-diffusion or fluid-like organic patterns" and "self-advection". So I expect swirling, smeared, organic streaks that slowly spiral and drift upward, not crisp geometry and not a symmetric kaleidoscope (the mirror score is 0). The one drawn element is the built-in wave, mode 7 (two_channel_parallel_traces). It is two horizontal waveform lines (left and right channel), centred at x=0.5, y=0.5 with line angle 0, offset from each other by 0.25 on the normal axis. So one line sits above centre and one below, each wiggling with the audio. They are thick, drawn as lines and not dots, with normal blending at alpha 0.303, so they are translucent. Because of the feedback they leave smeared, rotating, upward-drifting copies of themselves rather than staying as clean lines. A thin dark edge frame also exists: the outer border covers about 1% of the screen and blends black at alpha 0.8; the inner border has 0% coverage.

Colour. The wave colour is a source-level fact: r runs 0.2-0.8, g runs about 0-1, b runs 0.5-1.1, and the colour is clamped and then max-normalised so the brightest channel is 1. At unit audio bands the values are about r 0.42, g 0.29, b 1.07, which normalises to roughly (0.39, 0.27, 1.0), a saturated blue to blue-violet. Blue is the largest channel at that point and across most of its range, so I expect the lines to read as blue or violet, drifting toward magenta or cyan as the red and green terms swing. These terms depend on the volume input, so the hue shifts with the music. The recipe marks this as a raw source curve, not an observed colour. The final screen is also tinted by the composite hue shader: it multiplies by four corner shades, each normalised to a maximum of 1 and ranging 0.67-1 per channel, cycling with periods of about 14.6 s (r), 19.6 s (g) and 16.2 s (b). That gives a slow, soft, location-dependent tint shift across the screen. The corner placement is random per load and the recipe does not fix it. The recipe's steady-state hue family is "grayscale" (display about 0.0125, 0.0122, 0.0129), but it says that is only a lower bound. The calibrated display estimate gives mean saturation about 0.46 (interval 0.18-0.73) and warm/cool about 0.02 (interval -0.32 to +0.37), so the overall tone is roughly neutral between warm and cool, with moderate colour. I would describe the blue-leaning lines over a muted, tinted background and would not name a specific background hue. The warp and composite palettes are marked unresolved.

Motion. The motion is constant and steady, with no audio-driven jumps. There is a continuous slow spiral (0.02 rad rotation per step) combined with a fast upward drift of about 115 px/s and a barely noticeable inward contraction. The calibrated estimate gives a mean flow speed of about 0.15 screen widths per second (interval 0.05-0.32), a mean frame difference of about 0.038, and a p95 speed estimate of about 0.43 viewport widths per second (interval 0.30-0.67, partly assumed). That reads as flowing and smooth. The recipe's "energy" block gives smoothness 86 and intensity about 32, tagged "Smooth" and "Organic / Liquid". The two wave lines wiggle with the audio waveform each frame. The recipe finds no audio-triggered switch site, so I expect no sudden cuts or pattern changes.

Brightness and contrast. The calibrated mean luminance is about 0.28 (interval 0.09-0.52), so a mid-dark to medium image. The composite stage is a strong contrast stretch: on flat canvas levels it maps 0.25 to about 0.46 grey, and 0.5 and above to near white. So any canvas content above a modest level shows as bright, and I expect bright blown-out smears against darker regions rather than a uniformly dim picture. The activity estimate is 0.39, band "Normal" (the interval 0.01-0.84 also allows Chill or Intense). The black outer border adds a thin dark frame. Global brightness jumps are expected to be rare (about 0.46 per 10 s, interval 0-4.4).

Audio response. The waveform line shape follows the left and right audio waveforms continuously. The warp shader reads bass, mid and treble into all three colour channels (nine routes), but each is threshold or clamp based and the recipe gives no linear gain. I therefore expect the tint and brightness of the smears to shift with the music, but I cannot say how strongly or on which beats. The wave colour also depends on volume. The estimated bass effect on RGB is small (about 0.035, assumed), and the coherent flash estimate (about 2.8-4 Hz, delta peak about 0.1) is an assumption, not a measurement. I expect modest tint and brightness variation tied to the music, not a strobing or beat-flashing screen.

Uncertainty. Feedback decay, stored-level floor and trail persistence are all null, so I cannot say how long the line trails last or how dense the smear gets; that matters a lot for how busy the picture looks. The warp and composite palettes are unresolved, and the recipe says the actual palette depends on sampled image content. The composite tint corners are random per load. The wave colour terms and how much the wave dominates the final image after advection are only source-level estimates. All display numbers come from a source-calibrated estimate with wide intervals. Luminance could be anywhere from dark to fairly bright, and the activity band could be Chill, Normal or Intense. The recipe is an approximate description of a similar effect, not a pixel-exact specification. Nothing here comes from a rendered image.
